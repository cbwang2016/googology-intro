## 1. Proof-theoretic ordinal 证明论序数 (ch.15, pp.585–603)

### 1.1 Definition as the book gives it
- **Transfinite induction 超穷递归/超限归纳 TI(α)** (eq. 15.8, p.585):
  `TI(α): ∀β<α (∀γ<β ψ(γ) → ψ(β)) → ∀β<α ψ(β)`, for all **elementary computable predicates 初等可计算谓词** ψ.
- **Gentzen's theorem (Thm 15.2):** for every α<ε₀, PA ⊢ TI(α). But PA ⊬ TI(ε₀).
- **Definition (p.586):** "the ordinal α=ε₀ marks the provability limit 可证极限 of PA. We call it PA's proof-theoretic ordinal (Proof Theory Ordinal, PTO)": **PTO(PA)=ε₀**, also written |PA|=ε₀ (eqs. 15.9–15.10).
  More generally, PTO(T) is the supremum of the α for which T proves TI(α). "Transfinite induction above the PTO is unprovable."
- The book proves PTO(PA)=ε₀ in outline (pp.586–590):
  - **Lower half:** encode Cantor normal forms <ε₀ as natural numbers via prime-power coding ⟨k₁…kₙ⟩ = p₁^{k₁+1}…pₙ^{kₙ+1}. Then (ε₀,+,·,ω^·,<) ≅ a recursive structure (A₀, +̂, ·̂, ω̂, ≺), and each TI(α) for α<ε₀ is provable in PA.
  - **Upper half:** Gentzen sequent calculus 序列演算. Embed PA into the infinitary system PA_ω (ω-rule, ω规则). PA proofs get height < ω+m and finite cut rank. Cut elimination 切割消除 (Thm 15.6) gives height ω^{ω^{…^α}} < ε₀. So TI(ε₀) ⊢ Con(PA) (15.55). By Gödel II, PA ⊬ TI(ε₀).
- **General facts stated:** the stronger the theory, the larger its PTO. Every axiom system is recursive, so every PTO is < ω₁^CK (第一个非递归序数) (p.586). The field that computes PTOs and builds notations for them is **ordinal analysis 序数分析**. Names the book cites: Buchholz, Rathjen, Jäger, Stegert, Arai. OCFs 序数折叠函数 were invented for this work.

### 1.2 Intuition (book's framing plus a classroom gloss)
- Gödel incompleteness gives an "unnatural" unprovable sentence ("I am unprovable"). The book asks (p.585) whether *natural* statements are always decidable, and at what level they stop being decidable. The PTO answers: a theory T can "see" well-orderedness only up to PTO(T).
- **Goodstein's theorem** needs TI(ε₀), so it is unprovable in PA but true in ℕ (p.585 and p.812).
- **Notation link (p.586):** proving that a notation with limit α is well-ordered needs TI(α). So it must be done in a theory with PTO > α. Conversely, if a notation is proved well-ordered in T, its strength is < PTO(T).
- **BMS remark:** the BMS well-ordering proof uses all Σₙ-stable ordinals, so "people conjecture BMS might reach PTO(Z₂)". The book stresses this is not established, because BMS might already be provable in a much weaker system.
- **ZFC remark:** a notation of strength PTO(ZFC) would have termination unprovable even in ZFC (p.586).
- Classroom gloss: think of PTO(T) as "the longest countdown T can prove always finishes".

### 1.4 Provably total functions vs. f_{PTO(T)}: exact phrasing and caveats
The book never states one general theorem of the form "every provably total function of T is dominated by f_{PTO(T)}". It argues for it with an example, §15.7 "证明论与大数函数" (pp.601–603), using Friedman's tree(n):

1. Thm 15.11: totality 完全性 of tree(n) ⇔ Π¹₁-uniform reflection 一致反射原理 of ACA₀+Π¹₂-BI.
2. Thm 15.13: "ACA₀+Π¹₂-BI proves totality of all f_α(n) with α < ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω}), but cannot prove totality of f_{ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω})}(n)." Reason given: for too large α the system cannot prove α is an ordinal, and "this unprovable limit is exactly the PTO".
3. Thm 15.14: totality of tree(n) is not provable in ACA₀+Π¹₂-BI.
4. **Thm 15.15: "tree(n) grows faster than every function whose totality ACA₀+Π¹₂-BI can prove."**
   The book immediately warns: *"this is the riskiest step of the whole argument, so it is really only a conjecture 猜测"*. A function being unprovably total does not imply it grows fast, let alone that it dominates all provably total functions. Empirically, though, "if an unprovably-total function really is fast, it almost always dominates them".
5. The lower bound (15.16) and upper bound (15.17–18, via an ordinal assignment to trees below ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω})) combine into Thm 15.19: tree(n) has growth rate ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω}). The book adds that the upper bound is also "not reliable enough": the order type of the trees does not automatically bound the function. Its verdict: "googology is inherently non-rigorous… but backed by proof theory this is more rigorous than many googology results" (p.603).

Other places the book states the principle:
- Opening of part 6 (p.783): functions from independence results "often have growth rate equal to the PTO of the system" ("常常等于"). Caveats: these results "have not been sufficiently checked", and "**independence of a finite statement does not necessarily imply fast growth**". Some statements are finite but unbounded and must be further restricted to give a real big-number function.
- Precise, rigorous instances quoted:
  - KM(n) and KS_{ε₀} "grow faster than every PA-provably-total function" (Thm 20.10, 23.6).
  - KS_{Γ₀} does the same for ATR₀ (Thm 23.7).
  - **Arai's Mahlo hydra (Thm 22.1, p.914): "every provably total recursive function of KPM is dominated 控制 by some n↦F[H₀+n,∅]"**, and conversely each such function is KPM-provably total.
  - Thm 23.1–23.2 (descent-recursive functions 下降递归函数): f is provably total in PRA+TI(<A) iff it is A-descent recursive, and the growth sup is α.
  - MAH/SRP stable-order functions are cofinal in the provably recursive functions (Thm 22.12–22.13).
- Counter-example the book gives (20.42, MAH 一致性 I): a function k(r) whose totality is *equivalent* to MAH but which only grows like ε₀. The book: "this shows how incompleteness-functions can behave". This is a nice warning for students.
- Suggested phrasing for the course:
  "For natural theories T, the provably total recursive functions of T are exactly those eventually dominated by some f_α with α < PTO(T), for a *standard* fundamental-sequence system. Natural independent statements of T then tend to grow like f_{PTO(T)}. This is a theorem for PA (Wainer/Buchholz–Wainer) and many specific T, a heuristic in general, and depends on using 'nice' fundamental sequences."
  The Wainer/Buchholz–Wainer attribution is standard literature, not taken from these pages.

## 2. Natural big-number problems: rules, worked examples, growth rates

Tags: [verified] = I re-ran it in Python; [book] = book's claim; [std] = standard fact not in these pages.

### 2.1 Goodstein sequence Goodstein 数列 (§20.1, pp.807–812)
- **Hereditary base-b notation 以b为底的遗传记法:** write n in base b, then write every exponent (and exponent of exponent…) in base b too.
  Example: 100 = 2^{2^2+2} + 2^{2^2+1} + 2^2.
- **Rules (Def. 20.1–20.2):** G₀(n)=n. G_{k+1}(n) = B[k+2](G_k(n)) − 1, where B[a] means "write in hereditary base a, replace every a by a+1".
  **G(n)** := least k with G_k(n)=0 (the number of steps).
- **Worked values [book, verified]:**
  - G(0)=0, G(1)=1.
  - G(2)=3: 2 → 2 → 1 → 0.
  - G(3)=5: 3 → 3 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0. In hereditary form: 2+1 → 3 → 4−1=3 → 2 → 1 → 0.
  - G(4): 4=2² → 3³−1=26=2·3²+2·3+2 → 41=2·4²+2·4+1 → 60=2·5²+2·5 → 83=2·6²+6+5 → 109 → 139 → 173 → 211 → 253=2·11²+11 → 299=2·12²+11 → … → G₂₁(4)=2·23² → G₂₂(4)=24²+23·24+23 → …
    Its ordinal shadow: ω^ω → ω²·2+ω·2+2 → ω²·2+ω·2+1 → … → ω²·2 → ω²+ω·23+23 → … (eqs. 20.40–20.55).
- **Termination proof (Goodstein's theorem 定理20.1):** replace the base by ω. The resulting ordinals strictly decrease, ordinals are well-ordered, so the sequence reaches 0.
- **Exact formula [book]:** G(n) = H_{R^ω₂(n)}(3) − 3, where R^ω₂(n) means "write n in hereditary base 2 and replace every 2 by ω", and H is the Hardy hierarchy.
  - G(4) = H_{ω^ω}(3) − 3 = f₃(3) − 3. With f₂(n)=n·2ⁿ this gives **G(4) = 3·2^402653211 − 3** [std; my arithmetic agrees with the book's formula].
  - G(5) = f₄(4)−3. G(8) = f_{ω+1}(3)−3. G(16) = f_{ω^3}(3)−3 (book's list).
  - General pattern: G(2↑↑n) = H_{ω↑↑n}(3) − 3 = f_{ω↑↑(n−1)}(3) − 3.
- **Growth:** G(n) ∼ ε₀ (20.134).
- **Independence:** unprovable in PA, because PA's provable well-orders stay below ε₀. Called "one example of Gödel incompleteness" (p.812, also p.585). (Kirby–Paris 1982 [std].)
- Variants in the book:
  - nested Goodstein 嵌套 (§21.3): ε₀, ε_ω, φ(2,0)
  - "迫敛" Goodstein GSW (§21.12): Γ₀, independent of ATR₀
  - fractal Goodstein 分形 (§21.23): BHO, independent of KP
  - Weiermann–Goodstein (§21.29): ψ(Ω_ω), Π¹₁-CA₀
  - marxen.c (Bignum Bakeoff runner-up) is a Goodstein variant: f_{ω^ω}(2↑↑500) < output < f_{ε₀+ω³}(10⁶) (p.930)

### 2.2 Kirby–Paris hydra 九头蛇 (§20.2, pp.813–818)
- **Rules (Def. 20.3/20.5):**
  - Start from a straight path ("直树") with n edges.
  - At step m, delete the **rightmost leaf**. If its parent is not the root, take the parent's subtree (after the deletion) and attach **m copies** of it to the grandparent, as extra siblings of the parent.
  - If the parent is the root, nothing regrows.
  - Repeat until only the root remains. **Hydra(n)** = number of steps.
- **Bracket form:** a tree is a bracket string; () is a leaf or bare root.
  Rule: delete the rightmost innermost "()". If the enclosing bracket is not the outermost, append m copies of the enclosing bracket's (new) content right after it.
- **Worked [book, verified]:**
  - Hydra(1)=1.
  - Hydra(2)=3: ((())) → (()()) → (()) → ().
    The book also prints "Hydra(2)=4" at (20.145). That is a typo: its own list ends at step 3.
  - **Hydra(3)=37:**
    (((()))) → ((()())) → ((())(())(())) → ((())(())()()()()) → … → ((())(())) [7] → ((())()×9) [8] → … → ((())) [17] → (()×19) [18] → … → () [37]
  - Ordinal labels: ω^ω, ω², ω·3, ω·2+4, …, ω·2, ω+9, …, ω, 19, …, 0.
  - Map: () = 0, (H₁…Hₙ) = ω^{H₁}+…+ω^{Hₙ}.
- **Why you always win:** the ordinal strictly decreases at every chop, even though the tree gets wider. Depth never grows. Same logic as Goodstein (p.815).
  - Hydra(4) = f'_{ω·2+4}(5) − 1 ≈ f_{ω·2+4}(4) (slightly larger).
  - Hydra(5) ∼ f_{ω^{ω·2+4}}(5), Hydra(6) ∼ f_{ω^{ω^{ω·2+4}}}(6).
  - **Hydra(n) ≈ f_{ε₀}(n), growth ε₀** (20.215–216). The book says this is also PA's PTO.
- **Independence from PA:** the book implies it via ε₀ = PTO(PA) and the parallel with Goodstein, but does not state the Kirby–Paris theorem explicitly in this section. Standard statement [std]: "every strategy kills the hydra" is true but unprovable in PA (Kirby–Paris 1982).
- **Fastest strategy (hydra(n)):** always cut the deepest head, and among equals the one with most siblings. hydra(4) ≈ 2.656×10^13597, hydra(n+1) ∼ hydra(n)·2^{hydra(n)}, growth ∼3 (tower-level). This makes a great contrast: strategy matters for speed but never for termination.
- **Connection:** the hydra expansion is *identical* to Primitive Sequence System 初等序列 (PrSS) expansion, e.g. (0,1,2,2,1) → (0,1,2,2,0,1,2,2,…). The book calls these "Hydra-type notations". This is a direct bridge to BMS.

### 2.3 Beklemishev's worm (§20.3, pp.819–821) — optional companion
- **Rules:** a worm is a list [W₀,…,Wₙ]. At round m:
  - if Wₙ=0, delete it;
  - else let k = the largest i<n with Wᵢ<Wₙ. Keep the good part g=[W₀..W_k]. Form the bad part b=[W_{k+1},…,W_{n−1},Wₙ−1]. The new worm is g + b repeated (m+1) times.
- Worm(n) := steps from [n] to []. **Worm(1)=3, Worm(2)=51** [book, verified].
- Ordinals: [1]=ω, [1,1]=ω², [2]=ω^ω, [3]=ω^{ω^ω}. **Worm(n) ∼ ε₀.** Same family as PrSS/BMS/Y-sequence ("Worm 型记号").

### 2.4 Other ε₀-level one-liners (ch.20)
- **Fuse / burning numbers 燃烧数 (§20.4):** the times you can measure with ropes that each burn in 1 hour.
  - Generated from 0 by a~b = (a+b+1)/2 when |a−b|<1.
  - f(n) = gap after n; g(n) = −log₂ f(n): g(0)=1, g(1)=3, g(2)=10, g(3)=1541023937. The book prints 154102393 once; the text gives 1541023937.
  - g ∼ ε₀; set has order type ε₀. Very accessible for high school.
- **Paris–Harrington (§20.8):** finite Ramsey with "relatively large" homogeneous set (|H| > min H). Unprovable in PA. f_{ε₀}(n−3) ≤ PH(n,8) ≤ f_{ε₀}(n−2).
- Kanamori–McAloon KM ∼ ε₀; Friedman's many ε₀ functions (§20.30–20.46).
- **Graham's problem 19.20 (p.793):** the book quotes the bounds 6 ≤ n ≤ F⁷(12), with F(n)=2↑ⁿ3. The modern lower bound is 13 [std], so the book is dated here.
- Pre-ε₀ problems (ch.19) are mostly ∼ω (Ackermann level) or ∼ω^ω (sqar, braids).

### 2.5 tree(n) and TREE(n) (§21.14, pp.868–882; also §15.7)
- **Embedding 嵌入 (Def. 21.48):** repeatedly delete a degree-1 vertex with its edge, or a degree-2 vertex while joining its two neighbours (homeomorphic/topological embedding 同胚嵌入). B embeds in A if A reduces to B this way.
  For TREE, colours must match. The book's wording is informal: it does not say "rooted" or "inf-preserving".
  Standard Friedman definition [std]: finite **rooted** trees; T_i ≤ T_j via an injective map preserving labels and infima (nearest common ancestors).
- **tree(n) (weak tree, Def. 21.46 / 15.1):** maximal length of a sequence of (uncoloured) trees with the k-th tree having **≤ n+k** vertices and no earlier tree embedding into a later one.
- **TREE(n) (Def. 21.47):** vertices coloured with ≤ n colours, the k-th tree has **≤ k** vertices, no earlier tree embeds into a later one.
- **Values [book]:** TREE(1)=1, **TREE(2)=3**. tree(0)=1, tree(1)=2, tree(2)=5.
  The book's otree passage says "otree(1)=3, same as tree", which conflicts with tree(1)=2. Use the figure values.
  - **tree(3) ≥ 3·2⁴⁸ − 8 = 844424930131960** (constructed sequence). Strict "largest-ordinal-first" ordering gives only 262140.
  - tree(4) > H_{ε_{ω²·2+1}}(2↑↑↑6)
  - tree(5) > f_{Γ₀}(Graham's number)
  - tree(1.0001n+2) > f_{SVO}(n) (n ≥ 3)
  - **TREE(3) > H_{φ(1@ω,3)·φ(1@ω)}(tree(tree(3)+1))** (p.882)
  - The book's own phrasing: "TREE(3) is one of the most famous big numbers… don't confuse it with tree(3), which may not be outrageously big".
- **Finiteness:** Kruskal's tree theorem (Thm 21.26): embedding is a well-quasi-order 良拟序 (no infinite descending chain and no infinite antichain). The finite form uses the k+n bound (Thm 15.8).
- **Strength:** Kruskal is unprovable in ACA₀+Π¹₂-BI (Thm 15.14; p.871). Its PTO = ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω}) = φ(1@ω) = **SVO**.
- **Growth rates the book states:**
  - **tree(n) ∼ φ(1@ω) = SVO = ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω})** (21.156; 15.19). Shown by an explicit ordinal assignment: (()()) ∼ ω, (()()()) ∼ ε₀, ((())(())(())) ∼ φ(1,0,0), (()()()()) ∼ φ(1,0,0,0), … , (()…()) → φ(1@ω).
  - **TREE(n) ∼ φ(ω@ω)** (extended/array Veblen φ). Lower bound by explicit ordinal assignment with k bracket types (k colours ~ φ(k−1@ω, …)); upper bound φ(ω@ω) cited from [1652].
    In θ/ψ terms this is ϑ(Ω^ω·ω) = ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω·ω}), matching the user's guess "θ(Ω^ω ω)" [std correspondence]. It is just above SVO, not "the small Veblen ordinal itself".
- Related functions:
  - tree_f restricted versions (Otter constant α ≈ 2.9558, c = 1/log₂α): r ≤ c gives PRA-provable; r > c gives unprovable in (Π¹₂-BI)₀. This is a sharp phase transition 相变.
  - otree (ordered trees): growth unknown.
  - sgap/wgap ∼ ψ(Ω_ω) (labelled/gap Kruskal).

### 2.6 Buchholz hydra (§21.27, pp.894–895)
- **Board:** a finite rooted tree. The root is labelled +. Other nodes have labels ν ≤ ω. Nodes directly above the root have label 0.
- **Move at round n:** cut a head (leaf) σ with parent τ; A⁻ is the tree after removal.
  1. Label 0 and τ is the root: result A⁻.
  2. Label 0 and τ is not the root: make n copies of τ (with its subtree) and attach them to τ's parent (Kirby–Paris move).
  3. Label u ∈ ℕ, u>0: let ε be the first node below σ with label v<u. Copy the subtree A_ε, change the copy's root label to u−1 and the copy of σ to 0. Replace σ by this copy (n irrelevant).
  4. Label ω: relabel σ as n+1.
- **Theorem 21.55:** every hydra dies after finitely many steps, but this is **unprovable in Π¹₁-CA + BI**.
  BH(x) uses the single-branch hydra R_x labelled +,0,ω,…,ω (x nodes). BH(x) = least k such that R_x(1)(2)…(k) is won, i.e. the number of rounds.
- **Ordinal assignment (21.369–385):**
  - (+)=0, (+(0))=1, (+(0(0)))=ω, (+(0(0(0))))=ω^ω
  - (+(0(1)))=ε₀, (+(0(1(1))))=ζ₀, (+(0(1(1(1)))))=Γ₀
  - (+(0(1(1(1(0))))))=SVO, (+(0(1(1(1(1))))))=LVO
  - (+(0(2)))=BHO, (+(0(ω)))=BO
- **Growth:** the book says "BH(n) ∼ ψ(ψ_ω(0))", "also the PTO of Π¹₁-CA+BI". But PTO(Π¹₁-CA+BI) = ψ(ε_{Ω_ω+1}) = **TFBO** (book 15.174, 16.83). CORRECTION: the book's "ψ(ψ_ω(0))" is MOCF notation for TFBO and is right; BH growth ≈ TFBO, not BO.
- The book distinguishes this from the "PSS Hydra" that is equivalent to 2-row BMS.

### 2.7 SCG / SSCG (§21.32, pp.904–906)
- **Minor embedding 子式 (Def. 21.128):** B embeds in A if B is obtained from A by deleting vertices or edges and contracting edges.
  Robertson–Seymour graph minor theorem 图子式定理: there is no infinite sequence of graphs in which no earlier graph is a minor of a later one.
- **SCG(n):** maximal length of a sequence of **subcubic 亚三次 (max degree ≤ 3)** multigraphs, the k-th with ≤ n+k vertices, none embedding (as a minor) into a later one.
  **SSCG(n):** the same, but simple graphs (no loops or multi-edges).
- **Values:** SCG(0)=6, SSCG(0)=2, SSCG(1)=5.
  - SSCG(2) ≥ 3·2^{3·2^95} − 8 ≈ 10^{3.5775×10^28}
  - **SSCG(3) ≥ TREE(3)**, indeed ≥ TREE^{TREE(3)}(3)
  - SSCG(13) ≥ f_{ψ(Ω_ω)}(2↑↑1000)
  - SSCG(n) ≤ SCG(n) ≤ SSCG(4n+3), so both have the same growth
  - **Open:** whether SCG(2) > TREE(3)
- **Growth:** the GMT has strength between ψ(Ω_ω) and roughly ψ(Ω_{ω^{ω^ω}}) (OCR "ψ(Ωωωω)"; cites [1722]).
  Ordering planar graphs alone already uses everything below ψ(Ω_ω), so **SCG, SSCG ≳ ψ(Ω_ω)** (the Π¹₁-CA₀ ordinal) and ≲ the GMT bound.
  Conjecture: exactly ψ(Ω_ω), if subcubic GMT has strength ψ(Ω_ω). The topological-minor version is proved to have exactly that strength. "Not yet sufficiently sure."

### 2.8 Loader's function (§22.6, pp.929–946)
- Origin: David Moews' **Bignum Bakeoff** (2001): a C program of ≤ 512 non-space characters on an idealised machine. Winner: loader.c. Output D⁵(99) = **Loader's number**.
- **What D(n) does:** it implements a parser, type-checker, normaliser and proof search for the **Huet–Coquand Calculus of Constructions 构造演算** (λPω) in 512 chars. Every well-typed term normalises (halts).
  D(n) walks all bit strings up to n. Each that encodes a well-formed derivation contributes its number, collected into a running result.
  Intuitively: "the largest number provable to exist in CoC in fewer than log₂ n steps".
  Loader's tweak drops a premise of rule 4 ("a conservative extension of CoC", per his comment). The book discusses Girard-paradox risk and judges it probably safe.
- **Lower-bound intuition:** Church numerals give f_{ε₀}. Church-encoded ordinals ord = ΠA:*.((nat→A)→A)→(A→A)→A→A give Hardy H_α inside λPω, reaching ε-numbers, Veblen, "maybe up to ψ(Ω^{Ω^Ω})".
  OCF encodings might reach ψ(ψ_I(0)) or even ψ(ε_{K+1}).
- **Growth (the book's "bound"):** **D(n) ∼ PTO(Z_ω)** (22.139). Rationale: λPω is intuitionistic, so it can only prove ordinals well-founded that it can construct. It is "at least as strong as higher-order arithmetic". The PTO of CoC is the ceiling, so "without uncomputable functions we likely can never reach Loader's growth rate".
  Note PTO(Z_ω) itself is only conjecturally located (§1.3 row). This is an estimate, not a proved upper bound.

### 2.9 Laver tables Laver 表 (§22.22, pp.970–973)
- **Definition (Def. 22.92):** on {1,…,2ⁿ} there is a unique binary operation ⋆ₙ with
  - a ⋆ 1 = a+1 mod 2ⁿ (with 2ⁿ ⋆ 1 = 1)
  - left self-distributivity 左自分配律 a⋆(b⋆c) = (a⋆b)⋆(a⋆c) ("shelf 架")
  - Only works for sizes 2ⁿ.
  - Recursive computation: a⋆(b+1) = (a⋆b)⋆(a+1), and row 2ⁿ is the identity.
- **Period p(n)** = period of the first row b ↦ 1⋆ₙb.
  Correct values [verified]: n=0..10 gives 1,1,2,4,4,8,8,8,8,16,16. The book's list (p(1)=p(2)=1, p(3)=2, …) is shifted by one.
  - p grows **extremely slowly**: it first reaches 32 at an astronomically large n.
  - q(n) = min{N : p(N) ≥ 2ⁿ} is its fast inverse: q(0..4) = 0, 2, 3, 5, 9 [book; consistent with the verified p].
  - Dougherty: qⁿ(1) > f_{ω+1}(⌊log₃ n⌋ − 1); q(5) > f₉(f₈(f₈(254))).
  - The book also says q's growth is "known to exceed BO" [115]. That is a strong claim citing a googology source. Treat it as unverified.
- **Large-cardinal link (what is conjectural):**
  - Laver used **axiom I3** (a nontrivial elementary embedding j: V_λ → V_λ). Under I3, iterates of j generate a free shelf F₁, the Aₙ are its finite quotients, and their inverse limit contains F₁ (Thm 22.14).
  - Laver then found a purely finite proof that ⋆ₙ exists and is unique (Thm 22.15).
  - **But p(n) → ∞ is only proved assuming I3.** So totality of q is proved only in ZFC+I3.
  - Book's (non-rigorous) bound: growth(q) ≤ PTO(ZFC+I3).
  - Whether p(n) → ∞ is provable in ZFC (or PA) is **open**.
  - Also under I3: π_n(1) ≤ π_n(2).
- **Laver Table Yarn (LTY):** an ordinal notation (Test_alpha0, 2023) built from Laver tables, well-ordered under ZFC+I2 or I1. Its LTYF growth is ≤ PTO(ZFC+I1), true growth unknown.

### 2.10 Higher-level headline facts (ch.22–24, skim)

## 3. Large cardinals 大基数 (ch.17, pp.725–747; inner models ch.18)

### 3.1 Why they matter for googology (p.725, the book's three reasons)
1. Large-cardinal axioms give very strong theories, hence enormous PTOs.
2. As strong theories, their minimal transitive models L_α give strong non-recursive ordinals α.
3. Inner-model theory suggests stronger non-recursive ordinals, hence large recursive ordinals via OCFs.

The OCF tables already use I (inaccessible), M (Mahlo), K (weakly compact) as "collapsing cardinals". Their recursive analogues are the admissible ordinals 容许序数 / Π₂-reflecting, recursively inaccessible, recursively Mahlo, and Π₃-reflecting ordinals (pp.731–737).

### 3.2 Book's intro-level storyline
- **Axiom of infinity as the model:** ZFC without infinity can neither prove nor refute an infinite set. Large-cardinal axioms are "new axioms of infinity" that declare sets "beyond the theory" (p.725).
- **Comparison:** by consistency strength 一致性强度 (Thm 17.1). For two large-cardinal axioms, exactly one holds:
  - Con(ZFC+A₁) ↔ Con(ZFC+A₂), or
  - one strictly implies the other.
  This nearly gives a linear order.
- **Two ZFC ways to climb:** power set 幂集 (successor cardinals) and replacement 替换 (sups of sequences: ℵ_ω, ℵ_{ℵ_ω}, …, ℵ-fixed points). Even the fixed point ℵ(1,0) = sup{ℵ₀, ℵ_{ℵ₀}, …} has cofinality ω.
- **Cofinality 共尾数** cf(α) = shortest increasing sequence reaching α.
  - Regular 正则 means cf κ = κ; singular 奇异 means cf κ < κ.
  - Successor cardinals are regular. Singular cardinals are limits.
- **Inaccessible 不可达基数 (Def 17.3–17.4):** an uncountable cardinal that is regular AND a limit (weakly) / not reachable by power sets (strongly). Equal under GCH.
  - It "unites two seemingly contradictory properties", unreachable both by power set and by replacement from below. It is the analogue of ℵ₀ over the finite numbers.
  - ZFC cannot prove one exists (Axiom 17.2 declares it).
  - Hierarchy: α-inaccessible (1-inaccessible = κ with κ inaccessibles below it), via the Λ operator.
- **Mahlo 马洛基数 (Def 17.9):** introduces club 闭无界集 (closed + unbounded, like closed intervals and unbounded intervals on ℝ) and stationary 稳定集 (meets every club).
  - M is Mahlo if the inaccessibles below M form a stationary set.
  - A Mahlo cardinal is M-inaccessible (Thm 17.14), and that is "only a very weak property of it".
  - Hierarchy: α-Mahlo, hyper-Mahlo.
  - Equivalent: every f: κ→κ has an inaccessible closure point γ<κ (Def 17.12).
  - Recursive analogue: recursively Mahlo = Π₂-reflecting on admissibles.
- **Weakly compact 弱紧致 (Def 17.14):** κ → (κ)² (Ramsey property for uncountable κ), equivalently inaccessible + tree property, equivalently Π¹₁-indescribable.
  - Also: Erdős cardinals η_α, Ramsey cardinals κ → (κ)^{<ω}.
  - Indescribable 不可描述 (Def 17.24): Σ¹₁-indescribable = inaccessible.
  - Analogy: Π_n-reflecting ordinal ↔ Π¹_{n−2}-indescribable. Π₃-reflecting = "recursively weakly compact".
- **Measurable 可测基数 (§17.7):** motivated by length and probability as measures (μ(∅)=0, monotone, σ-additive, points have measure 0).
  - Lebesgue measure cannot be defined on all subsets (Thm 17.32).
  - A nontrivial σ-additive measure forces an inaccessible-sized set (Thm 17.34). Two-valued measures are ultrafilters 超滤.
  - κ is measurable if there is a (normal) κ-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter on κ.
  - **Equivalent (Thm 17.48):** κ is the critical point 关键点 of an elementary embedding 初等嵌入 j: V → M (M transitive).
  - Measurable ⇒ Π²₁-indescribable. **A measurable exists ⇒ V ≠ L** (Thm 17.50; Scott [std]), "a milestone".
- **Above measurable (§17.8–17.10):** strong, Woodin, superstrong, strongly compact, supercompact, extendible, huge, …
  - Rank-into-rank axioms I3, I2, I1, I0: "the strongest not yet proved inconsistent with ZFC".
  - Kunen: there is no nontrivial j: V → V in ZFC (Thm 17.75). Reinhardt and Berkeley cardinals contradict AC.
- **Ch.18 (inner models) headlines:** L, 0♯, L[U], 0†, Dodd–Jensen core model, Woodin's Ultimate-L conjecture, exacting cardinals.
  Exacting cardinals 正合基数 (Thm 18.105): exacting + extendible below it refutes the weak HOD and weak Ultimate-L conjectures. An *ultraexacting* (极正合) cardinal has the same consistency strength as an I0 embedding (Thm 18.110).

### 3.3 How to pitch the connection in the course
- Ladder analogy: finite → ω (axiom of infinity) mirrors accessible → inaccessible (new axiom). In both cases "you can't reach it from below".
- Recursive shadow: OCFs use Ω = ω₁^CK (or ℵ₁), I, M, K as "big things to collapse". Bigger cardinals in the OCF mean bigger countable ordinals, which means faster f_α.
- Laver tables show a large cardinal (I3) proving a *finite combinatorial* fact nobody can yet prove otherwise.

## 4. Open problems mentioned in these parts
1. Whether **BMS reaches PTO(Z₂)** (conjectured, no reliable evidence) (p.586, p.596).
2. **PTO(Z₂) = ψ_Ω(I_ω)** is the current frontier of ordinal analysis. PTO(Z₃), …, Z_ω and **PTO(ZFC) are unknown**. Conjectures: L_{α+1}'s ω_n ↔ PTO(Z_{n+1}); worldly cardinal ↔ PTO(ZFC).
3. Whether ordinal-analysis notations can be simplified without losing strength or well-foundedness. How they compare with googology notations (p.605).
4. The "domination step" (Thm 15.15) and the tree/TREE growth upper bound are heuristic, not rigorous.
5. **SCG/SSCG exact growth unknown** (between ψ(Ω_ω) and the GMT bound). **SCG(2) vs TREE(3) unknown.** Exact proof-theoretic strength of GMT and of subcubic GMT unknown.
6. otree growth unknown. Growth of many Friedman functions is only conjectured (part 6 opening caveat).
7. **Laver tables:** is p(n) → ∞ provable without I3 (in ZFC, or even PA)? The general formula for p⋆ₙq and periods is unknown. LTY's true growth is unknown.
8. Loader: CoC/Loader-extension proof-theoretic ordinal and exact placement of PTO(Z_ω).
9. Graham's problem: exact value unknown (book: 6 ≤ n ≤ F⁷(12); modern lower bound 13).
10. Set theory: Ultimate-L and HOD conjectures; whether I0-level axioms are consistent. Consistency of exacting + extendible together.
