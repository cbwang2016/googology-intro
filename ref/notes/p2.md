# 《大数理论》Part II 经典序数记号 — Study Notes (book pp. 117–254)

Source: `ref/parts/p2_veblen_ocf_pss.txt` (text extraction). Where superscripts/subscripts were ambiguous in the text dump, I checked the rendered PDF pages (book pp. 168, 206–207, 215, 219–221, 232). Equation numbers (4.x / 5.x / 6.x) are the book's.

Chapters: **Ch.4 Veblen 函数** (4.1–4.7), **Ch.5 序数折叠函数 OCF** (5.1–5.8), **Ch.6 PSS Hydra** (6.1–6.2). Sections marked `*` in the book are optional.

Notation used here:
- `ϕ` is the ordinary Veblen function. The book writes weak Veblen as `φ` (section 4.7) to tell them apart.
- `α[n]` is the n-th term of the fundamental sequence (基本列). `sup{...}` is the supremum (上确界).
- "`1st α↦f(α) fp.`" is the book's fixed-point notation (fp. = fixed point 不动点), and "`fp. aft β`" means "the next fixed point after β".
- `ψ^M` = Madore OCF (MOCF), `ψ^B` = Buchholz OCF (BOCF), `ψ^H_k` (also written `p_k`) = PSS Hydra function.
- **If the book doesn't say which OCF, it means MOCF.**

## 0. Big picture (the book's narrative arc)

1. **Ch.4:** In Part I, ordinal notation stopped at ε₀. To go further, take an increasing map, iterate it, and when you reach a **fixed point** (不动点) take the successor to jump out. Enumerating fixed points gives new, stronger maps, and labelling those levels gives the Veblen functions (一元 → 二元 → 多元 → 序元 → 扩展序元). The landmarks are ε₀ → ζ₀ → η₀ → ϕ(ω,0) → Γ₀ → Ackermann → SVO → LVO → BHO. The chapter then admits that "the Veblen extensions are too complex and weaker than expected", so it switches method.
2. **Ch.5:** To get bigger *recursive* ordinals, bring in a stronger object, the **non-recursive ordinal Ω**, and **collapse** it back down with an OCF ψ. This mirrors how FGH collapses ω to natural numbers. The chapter covers:
   - ψ with only recursive arguments gets stuck at ζ₀;
   - adding Ω reaches BHO;
   - Ω₂, Ω₃, … give BO;
   - ordinal subscripts give TFBO, BIO and EBO;
   - BOCF as an alternative normalisation, and where BOCF and MOCF catch up (追平) with each other;
   - FGH and SGH catch up at BO.
3. **Ch.6:** Strip the OCF down to its recursive core, "find the bad root, copy the bad part" (the **Hydra / Worm** structure). This gives the **PSS Hydra** and the equivalent **2-row BMS**. Both have limit **BO = ψ(Ω_ω)**.

## 1. Section-by-section summary (book order, with motivation)

### Ch.4 Veblen 函数

#### 4.1 序数不动点 Ordinal fixed points (p.99–103)
- **Motivation:** with natural numbers we built big numbers by iterate → diagonalise (successor → + → × → ^ → …). Doing the same with ordinals behaves differently, because iterating a map *reaches a ceiling*: the limit is absorbed by the map. Example: iterating `α↦1+α` from 0 gives ω, and 1+ω = ω, so **ω is the first fixed point of α↦1+α** (`ω = 1st α↦1+α fp.`, eq. 4.8).
- More examples:
  - `α↦ω+α` has first fixed point ω² (4.13).
  - `α↦ω·α` has first non-zero fixed point ω^ω (4.18). The trivial fixed point 0 is excluded.
- **Starting point doesn't matter.** Starting anywhere below ω^ω (e.g. ω+2 or ω³·3+ω) still ends at ω^ω (4.19–4.22).
- **Jumping out** (跳出不动点): take the successor ω^ω+1 and iterate again. This gives the 2nd fixed point ω^ω·2 (4.27). In general the β-th fixed point of `α↦ω·α` is ω^ω·β (4.33). Taking ω^{ω²} gives a "doubly fixed point" (二重不动点, 4.35).
- **General scheme for an increasing f** (4.36–4.46):
  - α₀ = sup{α, f(α), f(f(α)), …} is the 1st fixed point.
  - α₁ = sup{α₀+1, f(α₀+1), …} is the 2nd.
  - α_ω = sup αₙ.
  - In general **α_β = (1+β)-th fixed point**.
  - Footnote: counting from 0 ("0th fixed point") would be more natural, but the book keeps the conventional count starting at 1.
- **Fixed point vs limit point (不动点与极限点):** the limit α₀ of the iterates is a fixed point only if **f(α₀)[n] = f(α₀[n])** (4.49), i.e. f is continuous. Counterexamples: α↦α+1 has no fixed point, and α↦α+ω, α·ω, α^ω give limit points that are not fixed points (4.54–4.57).
- **Theorem 4.1 (Veblen fixed-point theorem):** if f: Ord→Ord satisfies (1) α<β ⇒ f(α)<f(β), and (2) f(β) = sup_{α<β} f(α) for every limit β, then f has arbitrarily large fixed points α = f(α). (These are the conditions for a *normal function*.)
- **Strategy for the rest of the chapter:** iterate to a fixed point → take the successor to jump out → repeat. When this stalls, turn the whole fixed-point enumeration into a stronger map f′ and repeat with that.

#### 4.2 Cantor 序数 (ε, ζ, η) (p.103–113)
- **ε numbers:** iterate α↦ω^α from 0 to get ε₀ = sup{0,1,ω,ω^ω,…}, which satisfies ω^{ε₀} = ε₀ (4.60–4.62). The book calls ε₀ the **小Cantor序数 Small Cantor Ordinal (SCO)**.
  - Jump out: ε₁ = sup{ε₀+1, ω^{ε₀+1}, …} = `α↦ω^α fp. aft ε₀`.
  - Then ε_ω = sup εₙ, and so on up to ε_{ε₀}, ε_{ε_{ε₀}}, … (4.63–4.98).
- **Standard forms and fundamental sequences of ε numbers:** Def 4.1 and Def 4.2, given in §2 below.
- **A second definition of ε_{α+1}:** the first fixed point of β↦ε_α^β, with FS ε_{α+1}[0]=0, ε_{α+1}[n+1]=ε_α^{ε_{α+1}[n]} (4.131–4.134).
  - The book *proves* both definitions give the same ordinal: ω^ω^{ε_α+1} = ε_α^{ε_α^ω}, and the two sequences interleave (4.137–4.143).
  - The two FSs behave differently in FGH, and fine FGH analysis is sensitive to this. The book uses the first definition from here on.
  - Footnote 3: the two FSs correspond to the odd and even terms of a BMS expansion.
  - Footnote 2: if α is a fixed point of β↦ε_β, then ε_{α+1} = α^α^α^… (a tower of α's). This notation is used often later.
- **ζ numbers:** the reachable sequence 0, ε₀, ε_{ε₀}, … is the iteration of the **stronger map α↦ε_α**. Its limit is ζ₀ = `1st α↦ε_α fp.` (4.146–4.148), called the **Cantor序数 Cantor Ordinal (CO)**. Enumerating as before gives ζ₁ = `α↦ε_α fp. aft ζ₀`, then ζ_ω, …, ζ_{ζ₀}.
- **η numbers:** η₀ = `1st α↦ζ_α fp.` = sup{0, ζ₀, ζ_{ζ₀}, …}, called the **大Cantor序数 Large Cantor Ordinal (LCO)**. Footnote 4: some literature uses η₀ for BHO, but googologists use η₀ = ϕ(3,0).
- **Summary of the section (4.180–4.182):**
  - ε_α = (1+α)th fixed point of ξ↦ω^ξ;
  - ζ_α = (1+α)th fixed point of ξ↦ε_ξ;
  - η_α = (1+α)th fixed point of ξ↦ζ_ξ.

  "This can go on forever", which motivates putting an index on the *level* of the map.

#### 4.3 二元 Veblen 函数 Binary Veblen (p.113–122)
- **Motivation:** label the fixed-point hierarchy with an array-like notation (数阵型记号).
- **One-variable:** ϕ(α) = ω^α (Def 4.7).
- **Two variables with natural-number first argument** (4.188): ϕ(0,α)=ω^α, ϕ(1,α)=ε_α, ϕ(2,α)=ζ_α, ϕ(3,α)=η_α. The core rule is **ϕ(k+1,α) = (1+α)th fixed point of β↦ϕ(k,β)** (4.229). Def 4.8–4.12 give the FS rules, all with the same 3-case pattern (second argument 0 / successor / limit).
- **Transfinite first argument:**
  - ϕ(ω,0) = sup ϕ(n,0) is the first **common** fixed point of all α↦ϕ(n,α) (4.233–4.236). The book calls it the **超Cantor序数 Hyper Cantor's Ordinal (HCO)**.
  - ϕ(ω,α+1)[n] = ϕ(n, ϕ(ω,α)+1).
  - **Pitfall (4.240):** sup_n ϕ(n, ϕ(ω,0)+1) ≠ ϕ(ω, ϕ(ω,0)+1). The latter is far larger.
- **Unified rule (4.285), for α>0:** **ϕ(α,β) = (1+β)-th ordinal that is a fixed point of δ↦ϕ(γ,δ) for every γ<α.**
- Def 4.14 (standard form) and Def 4.15 (fundamental sequences) are given in §2.
- **Γ₀** = sup{0, ϕ(0,0), ϕ(ϕ(0,0),0), …} = `1st α↦ϕ(α,0) fp.` (4.287–4.288). This is the **Feferman–Schütte ordinal (FSO)**, the limit of binary Veblen.

#### 4.4 多元 Veblen 函数 Multi-variable Veblen (p.122–128)
- **Ternary:** ϕ(1,0,γ) = Γ_γ = (1+γ)th fixed point of α↦ϕ(α,0) (4.292).
  - Footnote 5: the structure between ϕ(1,0,0) and ϕ(1,0,1) is more complex than it naively looks, so it is easy to *overestimate* a notation when analysing it with Veblen.
  - Then ϕ(1,β+1,γ) is the (1+γ)th fixed point of δ↦ϕ(1,β,δ), ϕ(2,0,γ) comes from δ↦ϕ(1,δ,0), …, then ϕ(ω,0,0), and so on (4.297–4.330).
- **How to read the arguments:** the rightmost argument *counts* fixed points (the "(1+β)th"). The **first non-zero argument to its left** (α) decides *which map* is being enumerated, and everything to the left of α is frozen during the step. Successor means "fixed point of the predecessor's map". Limit means "common fixed point of the maps for the FS".
- **Ackermann Ordinal (AO)** = ϕ(1,0,0,0) = 1st fixed point of ρ↦ϕ(ρ,0,0) (4.332).
- **General form ϕ(#, α, Z, β)**, where # is any sequence and Z is a (possibly empty) string of 0s (4.342–4.345). Unified rule: **ϕ(#,α,Z,β) = (1+β)th ordinal that is a fixed point of δ↦ϕ(#,γ,δ,Z) for every γ<α.** Def 4.16 and Def 4.17 are in §2.
- **SVO 小Veblen序数 Small Veblen Ordinal** = sup{ϕ(1), ϕ(1,0), ϕ(1,0,0), …} (4.372).

#### 4.5 * 序元 Veblen 函数 Ordinal-position ("transfinitary") Veblen (p.128–134)
- **Position notation:** ϕ(α_n,…,α₀) = ϕ(α_n@n, …, α₀@0). Zero terms are omitted, e.g. ϕ(1,0,0,0,0) = ϕ(1@4).
  - Foreign sources prefer a 2-row matrix (first row = values, second row = positions). Chinese googology prefers the α@β form.
- **Only two terms matter at each step:** the last term γ@0 (the counter) and the first non-zero term to its left (the level).
- **Generalise positions to ordinals β:** ϕ(1@ω) = sup ϕ(1@n) = SVO.
  - The book warns that in α@β, **β loses its meaning as a "position"**. It is better seen as just a parameter, and only finitely many non-zero terms can ever be written.
- **Worked progression:** ϕ(1@ω,1@0), ϕ(1@ω,γ@0), ϕ(2@ω), ϕ(ω@ω), ϕ((ω+1)@ω), ϕ(1@(ω+1)), ϕ(1@(ω·2)), … (4.383–4.418). There is extra structure when the position β is a limit: (α+1)@β is a common fixed point over the δ@β[n].
- **Unified rule (4.424):** ϕ(#, α@β, γ@0) = (1+γ)th ordinal that is a fixed point of ρ↦ϕ(#, δ@β, ρ@σ) for all δ<α, σ<β.
- Def 4.18 (standard form) and Def 4.19 (13 FS rules) are in §2.
- **LVO 大Veblen序数 Large Veblen Ordinal** = sup{0, ϕ(1@0), ϕ(1@ϕ(1@0)), …} = `1st α↦ϕ(1@α) fp.` (4.443–4.444).
- **Lesson stated by the book:** turning a variable from ℕ into ordinals ("transfinite extension") is usually a weak, *trivial extension*. In OCF terms, SVO→LVO is just ψ(Ω^Ω^ω) → ψ(Ω^Ω^Ω).

#### 4.6 * 扩展序元 Veblen Extended ordinal-position Veblen (p.134–137)
- The positions themselves get "carried" into arrays: ϕ(1@(1,0)) = LVO, then ϕ(1@(1,1)), ϕ(1@(2,0)), ϕ(1@(1,0,0)), ϕ(1@(1@ω)), ϕ(1@(1@(1,0))), …
- Different versions have different rules. The book presents **梅天狸 (Mei Tianli)'s rules**, a case analysis on the lowest non-zero term:
  - case 1: limit;
  - case 2: successor, with sub-cases where the position is a limit / successor / 0 / array;
  - case 3: a single argument, ϕ(α@0) = ω^α;
  - case 4: sums.
- Example table (4.474–4.483): "ϕ(1@(1,0),γ) enumerates the fixed points of ϕ(1@γ)", and so on.
- **BHO (Bachmann–Howard ordinal)** = sup{ϕ(1,0), ϕ(1@(1,0)), ϕ(1@(1@(1,0))), …} (4.484), the limit of the extended Veblen function.
  - Footnote 6: some proof-theory texts write BHO as η₀. Don't confuse this with the googology η₀ = ϕ(3,0).
- **The book's verdict:** extending Veblen further is too complex and "not as strong as hoped", so it moves to a different method (OCFs).

#### 4.7 * 弱 Veblen 函数 Weak Veblen (p.138–143)
- **Definition:** change only the last-argument rule to **φ(S, α+1) = φ(S, α) + 1** (4.485). The map α↦α+1 has no fixed points, so you continue straight on from limits. Weak Veblen appears inside some reflection and projection expressions later in the book.
- **Long correspondence table (4.487–4.660),** for example:
  - φ(1,0) = ϕ(1) = ω;
  - φ(1,0,0) = ϕ(1,0) = ε₀;
  - φ(1,0,0,0) = ϕ(2,0);
  - φ(1@φ(1,0)) = ϕ(ϕ(1),0);
  - φ(1@(1,0)) = ϕ(1,0,0) = Γ₀;
  - φ(1@(1,0,0)) = ϕ(1,0,0,0);
  - φ(1@(1@φ(1,0))) = ϕ(1@ϕ(1)) = ϕ(1@ω);
  - φ(1@(1@(1,0))) = ϕ(1@(1,0)) = LVO.

  In the extended-position range, weak Veblen is just one extra nesting level behind ordinary Veblen.
- **Catch point = BHO** (4.660). Enumerating limit points of the successor map is not fundamentally weaker than enumerating fixed points. You can swap in weak Veblen without changing the limit strength.

### Ch.5 序数折叠函数 Ordinal Collapsing Functions

#### 5.1 非递归序数 Non-recursive ordinals (p.145–147)
- **Motivation (the key philosophical step):**
  - FGH turns *recursive ordinals* into fast-growing functions, so the problem has become: get bigger recursive ordinals.
  - Repeat the trick one level up. Bring in something stronger than every recursive ordinal and map it back down.
  - A **recursive ordinal** (递归序数) is one obtainable bottom-up, i.e. it can be reduced to 0 by taking fundamental sequences and predecessors. Everything seen so far (Veblen etc.) is recursive.
- **Ω = sup{all recursive ordinals}** = sup{0,1,ω,ω^ω,ε₀,ϕ(1,0,0),SVO,LVO,BHO,…} (5.2). This is the **first non-recursive ordinal**.
  - Footnote 1: "recursive ordinal" in this chapter really means "recursive ordinal below Ω".
  - Footnote 2: this set exists and does not cause a Burali-Forti-type problem.
  - Footnote 3: older definitions take Ω = ω₁, the first *uncountable* ordinal. By today's standards ω₁ is "overpowered". The first non-recursive ordinal (ω₁^CK) is enough for an OCF.
- Then Ω+1, Ω·2, Ω², Ω^Ω, … Their sup is **Ω₂**, then Ω₃, Ω_ω = sup Ωₙ, Ω_{ε₀}, Ω_Ω, Ω_{Ω_Ω}, Ω_{1,0} = `α↦Ω_α fp.`, and so on (5.4–5.8).
  - Footnote 4: Ω_ω, and Ω_α for limit α, are strictly *not* non-recursive, because they have a recursive FS Ω_α[n].
- **OCF:** ψ(non-recursive-level ordinal) = big recursive ordinal (5.9). It "folds" higher structure into the recursive range.
  - Common OCFs below ψ(Ω_{Ω_{…}}) are **MOCF** and **BOCF**.
  - Above that level, googology usage is "very chaotic", with no unified version. The rigorous proof-theoretic OCFs are "barely understood by googologists".
  - **Caveat:** a real OCF is necessarily *defined set-theoretically* and is not a recursive notation. Googologists usually only write down the *collapsing behaviour* recursively, which gives a recursive notation that just "looks like an OCF" and loses the core idea.
- **Plan of the chapter:** (1) bottom-up behaviour; (2) recursive collapsing rules and FS definitions; (3) the set-theoretic definition and why it agrees with the rules.

#### 5.2 含递归序数的 Madore's OCF MOCF with recursive arguments only (p.147–154)
- **Enumeration:**
  - ψ(0) = ε₀;
  - ψ(α+1) = ψ(α)^ψ(α)^… (the limit of a tower of ψ(α)'s), so ψ(1)=ε₁, ψ(2)=ε₂;
  - ψ(α)[n] = ψ(α[n]) for limit α, so ψ(ω)=ε_ω.

  Hence **ψ(α) = ε_α = ϕ(1,α) for all α ≤ ζ₀** (5.25), and ψ(ζ₀) = ζ₀ is a fixed point of α↦ψ(α).
- **平台期 Plateau (key insight):** ψ(ζ₀+1) is *not* ε_{ζ₀+1}.
  - If ψ is your only tool, ζ₀ = sup{ψ(0), ψ(ψ(0)), …} is a *limit of finite nestings*. It is never available as a finished "brick", so you can't put "+1" inside the outermost ψ.
  - So **ψ(α) = ζ₀ for every recursive α > ζ₀** (5.30, 5.35). The output gets stuck at ζ₀, and only a non-recursive argument can push past it.
- **"Bricks" analogy with FGH (直观地构造OCF):**
  - f₀ is the first brick, f₁ is f₀ iterated n times, and so on.
  - Likewise ψ(0) is the smallest ordinal you can't build from {0,1,ω} with +,·,^ in finitely many steps.
  - ψ(1) may also use ψ(0) as a brick, and so on.
  - In general (Def 5.2), ψ(α) is the least ordinal not constructible from 0,1,ω, the ψ(β) with β<α where β is itself constructible, and +,·,^.
- **Set-theoretic definition (Def 5.3)** is in §2. The C^n(α) worked examples are:
  - C⁰(0) = {0,1,ω};
  - C¹(0) = {0,1,2,ω,ω+1,ω·2,ω²,ω^ω};
  - max Cⁿ(0) = a tower of (n+1) ω's (5.59–5.65).
  - ψ(1): C¹(1) adds ψ(0) (allowed since 0<1 and 0∈C⁰(1)), so max C²(1) = ψ(0)^ψ(0), and so on (5.66–5.72).
  - ψ(ω), ψ(ω·2), ψ(ω²), ψ(ψ(0)), ψ(ζ₀) (5.73–5.98).
  - ψ(ζ₀+1): you can only ever add ψ(ω), ψ(ψ(ω)), ψ(ψ(ψ(ω))), …, so it never reaches ζ₀, and ψ(ζ₀+1) = ζ₀ (5.100–5.105).

#### 5.3 含 Ω 的 Madore's OCF MOCF with Ω (p.155–173)
- **Why Ω:** ψ(Ω) is still ζ₀ (5.111), but ψ(Ω+1) = ψ(Ω)^ψ(Ω)^… = ε_{ζ₀+1} (5.112).
  - Ω, being beyond every recursive ordinal, **treats the infinite nesting ψ(ψ(…ψ(0)…)) as a single whole**. The book writes "Ω折叠了无限嵌套" as **Ω ⇒ ψ(ψ(…ψ(0)…))** (5.113).
- **Recurring pattern: stuck → fold with Ω → resume.**
  - **Addition:**
    - The run ψ(Ω+α) climbs until ζ₁ = `α↦ψ(Ω+α) fp.` and sticks there (5.127–5.131).
    - Folding with Ω+Ω gives ψ(Ω·2) = ζ₁, and then ψ(Ω·2+1) = ε_{ζ₁+1}.
    - This continues through ψ(Ω·3) = ζ₂ and ψ(Ω·ω) = ζ_ω, up to ψ(Ω·α) sticking at η₀ = ϕ(3,0) (5.166).
  - **Multiplication:**
    - Folding with Ω·Ω gives ψ(Ω²) = ϕ(3,0). Then ψ(Ω²·2) = ϕ(3,1), ψ(Ω³) = ϕ(4,0), and ψ(Ω^ω) = ϕ(ω,0), which is HCO (5.172–5.190).
    - Next come ψ(Ω^{ω+1}) = ϕ(ω+1,0) and ψ(Ω^{ψ(0)}) = ϕ(ε₀,0), up to ψ(Ω^α) sticking at Γ₀ (5.208–5.209).
  - **Exponentiation:** folding with Ω^Ω gives **ψ(Ω^Ω) = ϕ(1,0,0) = Γ₀** (5.214).
- **"All the fixed-point shapes at the Ω level have now been seen."** Continuing gives (5.215–5.296):
  - ψ(Ω^Ω·2) = ϕ(1,0,1);
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω+1}) = ϕ(1,1,0);
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω·2}) = ϕ(2,0,0);
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω²}) = ϕ(1,0,0,0);
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω}) = SVO;
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω^Ω}) = LVO;
  - **ψ(ε_{Ω+1}) = BHO**, where ε_{Ω+1} = Ω^Ω^Ω^… = `α↦Ω^α fp.` (5.297).
- **The unified folding rule (5.316–5.318):** for ∗ ∈ {+, ·, ^},
  `#∗Ω ⇒ #∗ψ(#∗ψ(#∗…))`, i.e. **ψ(#∗Ω) = sup{ψ(#), ψ(#∗ψ(#)), ψ(#∗ψ(#∗ψ(#))), …}**.
  - To expand, first "split off" the rightmost Ω so the tail looks like `#+Ω`, `#·Ω` or `#^Ω`. Worked example (5.319–5.325): Ω^{Ω^{Ω+1}·2}·3 is rewritten until it ends in `·Ω`. A nested example is ψ(Ω^Ω + Ω²·ψ(Ω^Ω·2)) (5.326–5.331).
- **递归共尾度 Recursive cofinality cf(α)** (Def 5.4): the least length of a *recursive* FS of α.
  - Successors have cf = 1, recursive limits have cf = ω, and Ω and the ordinals built from it have cf = Ω.
  - Footnote 5: for the true first non-recursive Ω, the true cofinality is ω, but such an ω-sequence is not recursive. That is why the book uses "recursive cofinality".
- **Recursive definition:** Def 5.5 (standard form) and Def 5.6 (FS rules with Ω[η] = η) are in §2. Worked FS of ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·3}) (5.360–5.362):
  - [0] = ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·2});
  - [1] = ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·2+ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·2})});
  - [2] = ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·2+ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·2+ψ(Ω^{Ω²+Ω·2})})}).
- **Set-theoretic definition (Def 5.8)** adds Ω to C⁰ (see §2).
  - **Why this works:** a "gap that can never be filled" (不可能被填满的空隙) separates the recursive ordinals from the ones involving Ω. So Ω never affects which *recursive* ordinal is the least one missing, until Ω is itself allowed as an argument (α > Ω).
  - Worked: C¹(Ω+1) may contain ψ(Ω) because Ω ∈ C⁰(Ω+1) and Ω < Ω+1, which gives ψ(Ω+1) = ε_{ζ₀+1} (5.369–5.375). The same mechanism explains the sticking points ψ(Ω+α) = ζ₁ and ψ(Ω·α) = η₀, and the escapes ψ(Ω·2+1) and ψ(Ω²+1) (5.383–5.433).
  - Footnote 6: for an MOCF with Ω only, taking Ω = BHO would already suffice. The book uses the first non-recursive ordinal so that **no future extension of the OCF ever reaches Ω**.

#### 5.4 含自然数下标的 Madore's OCF MOCF with Ωₙ (p.173–191)
- **Motivation:** stuck at BHO again. Every past breakthrough came from adding something bigger (ω for big numbers, Ω for the OCF), so add **Ω₂** together with a collapse **ψ₁** that outputs ordinals between Ω and Ω₂. Rename Ω = Ω₁ and ψ = ψ₀. **Inside ψ₁, Ω plays the role that ω plays inside ψ.**
- **ψ₁ enumeration:**
  - ψ₁(0) = ε_{Ω+1};
  - ψ₁(1) = ε_{Ω+2};
  - ψ₁(ω) = ε_{Ω+ω};
  - ψ₁(Ω) = sup_{α<Ω} ψ₁(α) = ε_{Ω·2};
  - ψ₁(Ω²) = ε_{Ω²};
  - ψ₁(ψ₁(0)) = ε_{ε_{Ω+1}};
  - ψ₁ sticks at ζ_{Ω+1}, and ψ₁(Ω₂) = ζ_{Ω+1} (5.439–5.473).
- **Back in ψ₀:** ψ(ψ₁(0)) = ψ(ε_{Ω+1}) = BHO (5.474). Continuing gives ψ(ψ₁(0)+1), …, ψ(ψ₁(0)+Ω), …, ψ(ψ₁(1)), … (5.475–5.489).
- **Subtle behaviour:**
  - In ψ(ψ₁(Ω)) = ψ(ψ₁(ψ(ψ₁(…)))), **the innermost Ω skips ψ₁ (where Ω is free) and expands against the outer ψ** (5.495).
  - ψ(ψ₁(ψ₁(Ω))) and similar terms follow the same rule.
- **ψ(Ω₂) and the "补层" (layer-renaming) rule:**
  - ψ(Ω₂) = ψ(ψ₁(Ω₂)) = ψ(ψ₁(ψ₁(…ψ₁(0)…))) (5.506–5.508).
  - Naively Ω₂ would expand against ψ₀ and give ψ(ψ(…)), which is wrong.
  - The fix: **when Ωₙ expands against a function with a smaller subscript ψ_m (m < n−1), the copied layers are renamed to ψ_{n−1}, while the original layer stays as it is.**
- Ω₃ with ψ₂, and so on, give **BO (Buchholz's Ordinal)** = sup{ψ(Ω), ψ(Ω₂), ψ(Ω₃), …} = sup{ψ(ψ₁(0)), ψ(ψ₂(0)), …} (5.550).
- **General folding rule (5.564–5.571), "找层 then 补层":**
  1. Start from the rightmost Ωₙ.
  2. Go outward to the **first function ψ_m with m < n** (the outermost function ψ₀ guarantees one exists).
  3. Write the expression as #₀(ψ_m(#∗Ωₙ)).
  4. Expand: #₀(ψ_m(#∗Ωₙ)) = #₀(ψ_m(#∗ψ_{n−1}(#∗ψ_{n−1}(…ψ_{n−1}(#)…)))). The copies are ψ_{n−1}. If # is empty, the innermost # becomes 0.
- **Worked examples (good for interactive "highlight the bad part" demos):**
  - ψ(Ω₅+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₃))): Ω₃ finds ψ₂, which is m = n−1, so no renaming is needed: → ψ(Ω₅+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₄+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₄+…))))) (5.551–5.557).
    - Book inconsistency: 5.551 prints the expression as ψ(Ω₅+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₃))), but 5.552–5.557 work with ψ(Ω₅+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₄+Ω₃))). With Ω₃ deleted, [0] = ψ(Ω₅+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₄))), then ψ(Ω₅+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₄+ψ₂(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₄))))), …. Use the 5.552 form.
  - ψ(Ω₅+ψ₁(Ω₄+ψ₃(Ω₃))): Ω₃ finds ψ₁, so the copies become ψ₂ (5.558–5.563).
  - ψ(Ω₃+Ω₂·2) = ψ(Ω₃+Ω₂+ψ₁(Ω₃+Ω₂+ψ₁(…))) (5.572–5.577).
  - ψ(Ω₃·ψ₁(Ω₃)) = ψ(Ω₃·ψ₁(ψ₂(ψ₂(…ψ₂(0))))) (5.578–5.583).
  - ψ(Ω₃·ψ₂(Ω₂^{Ω₂})) = ψ(Ω₃·ψ₂(Ω₂^{ψ₁(Ω₃·ψ₂(Ω₂^{ψ₁(…)}))})) (5.584–5.589).
  - ψ(ψ₅(Ω₅+ψ₁(Ω₄·ψ₃(Ω₃^{Ω₃})))) (5.590–5.596).
- Def 5.9 (standard form), Def 5.10 (cf), Def 5.11 (FS rules with an auxiliary γ-sequence) and Def 5.12/5.13 (set-theoretic) are in §2. The worked FS computations (5.619–5.627) reproduce the find-layer rule.
- **Hydra structure (5.697–5.699):**
  - The expansion #₀(ψ_m(#∗Ωₙ)) → copy ψ_m(#∗•) has exactly the shape of **PrSS** (初等序列): Ωₙ = last term (末项), ψ_m = bad root (坏根), #₀ = good part (好部), ψ_m(#∗•) = bad part (坏部).
  - Example: ψ(ψ₁(Ω+Ω)) = ψ(ψ₁(Ω+ψ(ψ₁(Ω+ψ(ψ₁(Ω+…)))))) has the same shape as (0,1,1,1) = (0,1,1,0,1,1,0,1,1,…).
  - PrSS is a 1-row Hydra. An OCF with natural-number subscripts is a 2-row Hydra: one row is the subscript, the other is the bracket nesting depth.
- **折叠层次 levels of collapsing:** ψ₂ collapses Ω₃, ψ₁ collapses Ω₂, ψ collapses Ω, and **FGH can be seen as "collapsing ω to a natural number"** (footnote 7 cites [922] on a category-theoretic relation). At BO the collapsing step has been repeated infinitely often, so the first layer (FGH) becomes irrelevant and the boundary between natural numbers and recursive ordinals "blurs".

#### 5.5 含序数下标的 Madore's OCF MOCF with ordinal subscripts (p.191–199)
- **Definitions:**
  - Ω_{α+1} = the first non-recursive ordinal after Ω_α; for limit α, Ω_α = sup_{β<α} Ω_β.
  - For limit α it is wrong to call Ω_α "the α-th non-recursive ordinal".
  - ψ_α(β) outputs a value in (Ω_α, Ω_{α+1}). It is sometimes written ψ_{Ω_α}, and the book notes that the full form will be needed later for reflection ordinals.
- **Folding rules:**
  - Successor subscript: same find-layer/rename rule as before.
  - Recursive-limit subscript: #(Ω_μ)[n] = #(Ω_{μ[n]}).
  - **Non-recursive subscript:** treat "subscript" as a new operation Ω_• and apply find-layer to it: #₀(ψ_ν(#∗Ω_μ)) = #₀(ψ_ν(#∗Ω_{ψ_μ(#∗Ω_{…ψ_μ(#∗Ω)})})) (5.711–5.714).
- **Landmarks:**
  - ψ(Ω_ω) = BO.
  - **TFBO (Takeuti–Feferman–Buchholz)** = ψ(ψ_ω(0)) = ψ(Ω_ω^{Ω_ω^{…}}) (5.725).
  - **BIO (Bird's Ordinal)** = ψ(Ω_Ω) (5.750).
  - **EBO (Extended Buchholz Ordinal)** = sup{ψ(Ω), ψ(Ω_Ω), ψ(Ω_{Ω_Ω}), …} (5.764). This is the subscript fixed point and the limit of Ω-level OCFs.
- Def 5.14–5.18 are in §2. Worked FS (5.789–5.794):
  - ψ(Ω_{Ω^Ω})[0] = ψ(Ω_ω);
  - ψ(Ω_{Ω^Ω})[1] = ψ(Ω_{Ω^{ψ(Ω_ω)}}).
- **The book's lesson again:**
  - In 2-row/higher BMS, BO = (0,0,0)(1,1,1) and EBO = (0,0,0)(1,1,1)(2,1,1)(3,1,0)(2,0,0) (5.810–5.811). These values are close, so EBO is only slightly bigger than BO.
  - Generalising a variable from ℕ to ordinals ("超限记号") gives limited gains. A real breakthrough needs a stronger *recursive core*.

#### 5.6 Buchholz's OCF (BOCF) (p.199–205)
- **Differs from MOCF only in the base case and the successor rule:** **ψ(0) = 1** and **ψ(α+1) = ψ(α)·ω**. Limits and folding (#∗Ω) are the same as in MOCF.
  - In standard BOCF only **addition** is used, since · and ^ can be expressed with higher ψ's. Out of habit the book still writes · and ^.
- **Early values:** ψ(1) = ω, ψ(2) = ω², ψ(ψ(1)) = ω^ω, … **ψ(Ω) = ε₀** (5.816–5.823).
- **With ψ₁** (ψ₁(0) = Ω, ψ₁(α+1) = ψ₁(α)·ω), there is a long table (5.824–5.871):
  - ψ(ψ₁(1)) = ψ(Ω·ω) = ε_ω;
  - ψ(ψ₁(ψ₁(0))) = ψ(Ω²) = ϕ(2,0);
  - ψ(Ω^Ω) = Γ₀;
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω^ω}) = SVO;
  - ψ(Ω^{Ω^Ω}) = LVO;
  - **ψ(Ω₂) = ψ(ψ₁(Ω₂)) = BHO**.
- **Higher levels:**
  - In BOCF, ψ(ψ₃(0)) and ψ(Ω₃) are the same ordinal (5.883), since ψ_ν(0) = Ω_ν.
  - BO = sup ψ(Ωₙ) = ψ(Ω_ω) (5.890–5.891);
  - EBO = ψ(Ω_{Ω_{…}}) (5.895).
- **Recursive definition (Def 5.20) and set-theoretic definition (Def 5.21)** follow Maksudov's scheme [926–927]. Buchholz's original definition (5.901–5.908) uses Ω_ν = ℵ_ν (Ω₀ = 1), the closure condition P(γ) ⊆ Cⁿ, and ψ_μ(ξ) with μ ≤ ω, and is much more complex. All are in §2.

#### 5.7 * BOCF 与 MOCF 的追平 Catching between BOCF and MOCF (p.205–210)
- **Def 5.22 (追平 catching):** ψ_A and ψ_B **catch up at α** if ψ_A(α) ≤ ψ_B(α) < ψ_A(α+1). α is the catching point (追平点).
- **Analogy:** ψ_A(α)=α and ψ_B(α)=2·α are "two runners", one taking 1 m steps and the other 2 m steps. They start together and separate, yet meet again at ω because 2·ω = ω (5.909–5.911).
- **Results:**
  - ψ^B(Ω) = ψ^M(0) = ε₀;
  - ψ^B(Ω·2) = ψ^M(1);
  - ψ^B(Ω²) = ψ^M(Ω);
  - ψ^B(Ω³) = ψ^M(Ω²);
  - in general **MOCF's Ω^α ≈ BOCF's Ω^{1+α}**;
  - they **catch up at ψ(Ω^ω)** and again at ψ(Ω^ω·2), …, ψ(ε_{Ω+1}).
- **Rule of thumb (book):** before BHO, if the OCF argument is a sum of terms ≥ Ω^ω with no smaller terms, BOCF and MOCF give the same ordinal.
- **With Ω₂:**
  - ψ^B(Ω₂) = ψ^M(ψ^M₁(0));
  - ψ^B(Ω₂²) = ψ^M(Ω₂);
  - ψ^B(Ω₂^ω) = ψ^M(Ω₂^ω).
- **General pattern below EBO:**
  - ψ^B(Ω_{α+1}) = ψ^M(ψ^M_α(0)) and ψ^B(Ω_{α+1}²) = ψ^M(Ω_{α+1});
  - for limit α, ψ^B(Ω_α) = ψ^M(Ω_α), so **BO and EBO are common values**.
- **Preview of I:** the 递归不可达序数 recursively inaccessible I satisfies ψ^B(I) = ψ^M(ψ^M_I(0)) = EBO (5.1045). I behaves *as if its subscript were a successor*, even though I = Ω_I. This leads into the later 反射序数 chapter.

#### 5.8 * FGH 与 SGH 的追平 Catching between FGH and SGH (p.210–216)
- **SGH (slow-growing hierarchy):** g₀(n)=0, g_{α+1}(n) = g_α(n)+1, g_α(n) = g_{α[n]}(n).
  - Values: g_m(n)=m, g_ω(n)=n, g_{ω·2}(n)=2n, g_{ω²}(n)=n², g_{ω^ω}(n)=nⁿ, g_{ε₀}(n) = n↑↑n.
  - **Below ε₀, replace ω by n.** SGH shows "how hard it is to construct" an ordinal.
- **SGH is very sensitive to the choice of FS.** The book uses ε₁ = sup{0,1,ε₀,ε₀^ε₀,…}. Values:
  - n↑↑n ~ ε₀;
  - n↑↑↑n ~ ζ₀;
  - n↑↑↑↑n ~ η₀;
  - n↑ⁿn ~ ϕ(ω,0).
  - Informal "ω↑ⁿω" in the literature means ϕ(n,0).
- **FGH ↔ SGH table:**
  - f_ω ~ ψ(Ω^ω);
  - f_{ω+1} ~ ψ(Ω^Ω) = Γ₀;
  - f_{ω^ω} ~ SVO;
  - f_{ψ(0)} ~ ψ(ψ₁(0));
  - f_{ψ(Ω)} ~ ψ(Ω₂);
  - f_{ψ(ψ₁(0))} ~ ψ(ψ₂(0));
  - f_{ψ(Ω₂)} ~ ψ(Ω₃).

  Rule: when the FGH index goes from a limit α to α+1, one ω inside the SGH-side OCF becomes Ω. Also, an FGH ordinal built from Ω_ν's corresponds to an SGH ordinal with Ω_ν → Ω_{1+ν} and every non-outermost ψ subscript raised by 1.
- **The first catching point is BO:** f_BO(n) ≈ g_BO(n), more precisely **f_BO(n−1) ≤ g_BO(n) ≤ f_BO(n+1)** (5.1155–5.1156).
  - **Meaning:** building a function with FGH rate BO starting from big numbers is exactly as hard as building BO starting from ω.
  - The book calls this unification of ordinal and big-number notations "the most profound shift in googology so far": from a big-number-centred paradigm to an **ordinal-centred** one.
  - Intuition: when the number notation is at Knuth arrows, the ordinal notation (n → ω) is a constant. At linear arrays it is one exponent, and at PrSS it is just an exponential tower.
- **Catching function C(α) (Def 5.23):**
  - C(0) = ψ(Ω_ω);
  - C(1) = ψ(Ω_{Ω_ω});
  - C(2) = ψ(Ω_{Ω_{Ω_ω}});
  - C(ω) = ψ(ψ_I(0)).

  It is only meaningful for recursive ordinals.

### Ch.6 PSS Hydra

#### 6.1 PSS Hydra (p.217–222)
- **PrSS recap (Def 6.1).** The book calls it the simplest **Worm-type notation**. Its core is: the last term separates good and bad parts, then delete the last term, keep the good part, and copy the bad part. The OCF expansion #₀(ψ_μ(#∗Ω_ν)) is the same pattern.
  - Example: ψ(Ω+ψ₁(Ω₃+ψ₂(Ω₂))) → ψ(Ω+ψ₁(Ω₃+ψ₂(ψ₁(Ω₃+ψ₂(ψ₁(…)))))) (6.4–6.6).
- **Motivation:** keep *only* this recursive core to get a notation of the same strength.
- **PSS Hydra** expressions are built from ψ^H_n, + and 0. Innermost "(0)" is usually dropped, and ψ^H_n is abbreviated pₙ. Example: ψ^H₁(ψ^H₂(ψ^H₃(0)+ψ^H₂(0))) = p1(p2(p3+p2)).
- **Rules (Def 6.2)** are in §2. The **Hydra mode** is "use ψ_{k+1} to expand the iteration inside ψ_k".
  - Apart from an overall constant +1, the subscripts follow the PrSS expansion exactly. But the nesting of brackets adds information, and that is what gives the extra strength.
- **Limit:** p1(p2(p3(p4(…)))) = ψ(Ω_ω) = BO (6.18, 6.99).
- **Theorem 6.1 (necessary, not sufficient):** in a standard expression, every ψ^H_{i+1} lies inside some ψ^H_i.
  - "Valid" (合法) means expandable by the rules. "Standard" (标准) means reachable from the limit expression by taking FSs. Valid is the wider of the two.
- **Big-number version (Def 6.3):** expand to the (n+1)-th term and change [n] to [n+1]. It has HH rate = the ordinal, and FGH limit ψ(Ω_ω). The book calls it "the easiest known way to get a BO-rate function".

#### 6.2 双行 BMS Two-row Bashicu Matrix System (p.222–233)
- **Format:** the 2×n matrix is written column by column, e.g. (0,0)(1,1)(2,2)(3,2)(2,0). Trailing zeros of a column may be dropped, but the very first 0 is kept, e.g. (0)(1,1)(2,2)(3,2)(2).
- **Definitions:**
  - parent (父项) for row 1 and row 2;
  - ancestor (祖先项);
  - bad root (坏根);
  - **difference vector 阶差向量 Δ**;
  - bad part / good part (坏部 / 好部);
  - expansion G B L → G B B₁ B₂ … with Bₙ = B + n·Δ (Defs 6.4–6.12, in §2).
  - Footnote 1: the book uses the "level-marking" convention for sequence notations. An entry's value says how many parent steps lead to a parentless entry. The alternatives mark the parent's position or distance.
- **Practical recipe for the bad root:** take the columns of the row-1 ancestors of the last row-1 entry as a sub-matrix. In it, find the first entry to the left that is smaller than the last row-2 entry. Its column is the bad root.
- **Three worked examples (6.104–6.138)** are in §3.
- **Successor case:** a last column of all zeros means "delete it and add 1", e.g. (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(0,0) = (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)+1. The empty matrix is 0. A computer can be used to help with expansions [455,448].
- **Limit:** (0,0)(1,1)(2,2)…. A standard expression is one reachable from the limit by taking FSs. Theorem 6.2 lists necessary conditions:
  - (1) the first column is all 0;
  - (2) in each column, row 2 ≤ row 1;
  - (3) every non-zero entry is at most parent+1.

  Derivation (6.144) shows that (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)(2,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1) is standard.
- **Analysis table (6.145–6.267):** see §4.
- **Correspondence with PSS Hydra (6.268–6.270):**
  - Each column is one ψ^H. **Row 2 = subscript − 1** and **row 1 = bracket depth.** Insert "(0)" where row 1 decreases and at the innermost ψ^H.
  - Row-1 ancestors correspond to the chain of enclosing ψ^H's. The extra row-2 condition ("the parent's upper entry must be an ancestor") skips same-level siblings. Δ row 2 = 0 means subscripts are unchanged when copying. Δ row 1 is the increase in bracket depth per copy.
- **Big-number version:** f(n) = S[n]. Expand to the (n+1)-th term and change [n] to [n+1]; the original version used n² for both, which makes almost no difference to growth. It has the same HH rate, and FGH limit ψ(Ω_ω).
- **Closing remark:** PrSS = 1-row BMS, with limit ε₀. 2-row BMS has limit BO. Arbitrary-row BMS (later in the book) is "extremely strong".

## 2. Exact formal definitions (as stated in the book)

### 2.1 ε-number standard form and FS (Def 4.1, 4.2)
The book doesn't use the phrase "Cantor normal form" in this Part. Its **标准形式 (standard form)** is the CNF condition (the ω-level rules come from Part I). "Cantor 序数" in this chapter is the name for ε₀/ζ₀/η₀ (SCO/CO/LCO), not CNF.

**Def 4.1 (standard form of ε ordinals):**
1. 0 is standard.
2. If α₁,…,α_k are standard, α₁ ≥ α₂ ≥ … ≥ α_k > 0, and each αᵢ is **additively principal** (加法主序数), then α₁+…+α_k is standard.
3. If α is standard and ω^α > α, then ω^α is standard.
4. If α is standard and ε_α > α, then ε_α is standard.

Additively principal: a non-zero α with β+α = α for every β < α.

**Def 4.2 (FS of ε ordinals):**
1. (α₁+…+α_k)[n] = α₁+…+α_k[n], for α_k a limit.
2. ω[n] = n.
3. ω^{α+1}[n] = ω^α·n.
4. ω^α[n] = ω^{α[n]}, for α a limit.
5. ε₀[0] = 0, ε₀[n+1] = ω^{ε₀[n]}. Explicitly, ε₀[n] is a tower of (n−1) ω's: ε₀[1]=1, ε₀[2]=ω, ε₀[3]=ω^ω.
6. ε_{α+1}[0] = ε_α+1, ε_{α+1}[n+1] = ω^{ε_{α+1}[n]}.
7. ε_α[n] = ε_{α[n]}, for α a limit.

"All ordinals involved are countable recursive ordinals; similarly below."

- **Def 4.3/4.4 (ζ):** add the rule "ζ_α standard if ζ_α > α", and the FS rules ζ₀[0]=0, ζ₀[n+1]=ε_{ζ₀[n]}; ζ_{α+1}[0]=ζ_α+1, ζ_{α+1}[n+1]=ε_{ζ_{α+1}[n]}; ζ_α[n]=ζ_{α[n]} for limit α.
- **Def 4.5/4.6 (η):** the same with ζ→η and ε→ζ.

### 2.2 Veblen functions
**Def 4.7 (one variable ϕ(α) = ω^α):** ϕ(0)=1; ϕ(α+1)[0]=0; ϕ(α+1)[n+1] = ϕ(α+1)[n]+ϕ(α); ϕ(α)[n] = ϕ(α[n]) for limit α.

**Def 4.12 (ϕ(k+1,α) for natural k):**
1. ϕ(k+1,0)[0] = 0, and ϕ(0,α) = ω^α.
2. ϕ(k+1,0)[n+1] = ϕ(k, ϕ(k+1,0)[n]).
3. ϕ(k+1,α+1)[0] = ϕ(k+1,α)+1.
4. ϕ(k+1,α+1)[n+1] = ϕ(k, ϕ(k+1,α+1)[n]).
5. ϕ(k+1,α)[n] = ϕ(k+1,α[n]) for limit α.

**Def 4.13 (ϕ(ω,α)):** ϕ(ω,0)[n] = ϕ(n,0); ϕ(ω,α+1)[n] = ϕ(n, ϕ(ω,α)+1); ϕ(ω,α)[n] = ϕ(ω,α[n]) for limit α.

**Fixed-point characterisation:**
- ϕ(α+1,β) = (1+β)th fixed point of γ↦ϕ(α,γ).
- For limit α, ϕ(α,β) = (1+β)th common fixed point of all γ↦ϕ(α[n],γ).
- Combined, for α > 0: **ϕ(α,β) = (1+β)th ∀γ<α (δ↦ϕ(γ,δ) fp.)** (4.285).

**Def 4.14 (standard form, binary):**
1. 0 is standard.
2. If each ϕ(αᵢ,βᵢ) is standard and ϕ(αᵢ,βᵢ) ≥ ϕ(α_{i+1},β_{i+1}), then ϕ(α₁,β₁)+…+ϕ(αₙ,βₙ) is standard.
3. If α, β are standard and ϕ(α,β) > α, ϕ(α,β) > β, then ϕ(α,β) is standard.

**Def 4.15 (FS of binary ϕ(α,β)):**
1. If ϕ(α_k,β_k) is a limit: (ϕ(α₁,β₁)+…+ϕ(α_k,β_k))[n] = ϕ(α₁,β₁)+…+ϕ(α_k,β_k)[n].
2. For limit α and any β: ϕ(0,α)[n] = ϕ(0,α[n]) and ϕ(0,β+1)[n] = ϕ(0,β)·n.
3. ϕ(α+1,0)[0] = 0.
4. ϕ(α+1,0)[n+1] = ϕ(α, ϕ(α+1,0)[n]).
5. ϕ(α+1,β+1)[0] = ϕ(α+1,β)+1.
6. ϕ(α+1,β+1)[n+1] = ϕ(α, ϕ(α+1,β+1)[n]).
7. For limit α: ϕ(α,0)[n] = ϕ(α[n],0).
8. For limit α: ϕ(α,β+1)[n] = ϕ(α[n], ϕ(α,β)+1).
9. For limit β: ϕ(α,β)[n] = ϕ(α,β[n]).

**Γ₀** = sup{0, ϕ(0,0), ϕ(ϕ(0,0),0), …} = 1st α↦ϕ(α,0) fp. (4.287–4.288).

**Def 4.16 (standard form, multi-variable):**
1. 0 is standard.
2. A decreasing sum of ϕ-terms is standard.

- **Ackermann ordinal** = ϕ(1,0,0,0).
- **SVO** = sup{ϕ(1), ϕ(1,0), ϕ(1,0,0), …} = ϕ(1@ω).

**Def 4.18 (standard form, ordinal-position):**

### 2.3 Madore's OCF (MOCF)
**Def 5.1 (recursive arguments only):**
1. ψ(0) = ε₀.
2. ψ(α+1)[0] = 0 for α ≤ ζ₀.
3. ψ(α+1)[n+1] = ψ(α)^{ψ(α+1)[n]} for α ≤ ζ₀.
4. ψ(α)[n] = ψ(α[n]) for limit α ≤ ζ₀.
5. ψ(α) = ζ₀ for every recursive α > ζ₀.

**Def 5.2 (informal):** ψ(α) = the least ordinal that cannot be built in finitely many steps from 0, 1, ω, all ψ(β), and +, ·, ^. Here β < α, and β itself must be buildable that way.

**Def 5.3 (set-theoretic, recursive arguments):**
1. C⁰(α) = {0, 1, ω}.
2. Cⁿ⁺¹(α) = { γ+δ, γ·δ, γ^δ, ψ(η) | γ, δ, η ∈ Cⁿ(α); η < α }.
3. C(α) = ⋃_{n<ω} Cⁿ(α).
4. **ψ(α) = min{β | β ∉ C(α)}** (5.109 writes min{β∈Ω | β∉C(α)}).

The text's Cⁿ⁺¹ formula lists only the new elements. The book's worked examples include Cⁿ in Cⁿ⁺¹ (e.g. C¹(0) ∋ 0, 1, ω), so read it as cumulative.

**Folding rule with Ω (5.316–5.318, 5.347–5.349):** with ∗ ∈ {+, ·, ^},
- ψ(#∗Ω) = sup{ψ(#), ψ(#∗ψ(#)), ψ(#∗ψ(#∗ψ(#))), …};
- ψ(#∗Ω)[0] = ψ(#);
- ψ(#∗Ω)[n+1] = ψ(#∗ψ(#∗Ω)[n]).

Plus ψ(α)[n] = ψ(α[n]) for recursive limits, ψ(α+1)[0] = ψ(α), ψ(α+1)[n+1] = ψ(α)^{ψ(α+1)[n]}, and ψ(0) = ε₀.

**Def 5.4 (递归共尾度 recursive cofinality):** cf(α) = the least length of a recursive FS of α. cf(successor) = 1, cf(recursive limit) = ω, cf(Ω and similar) = Ω.

**Def 5.5 (standard form, MOCF with Ω):**
1. If α₁ ≥ … ≥ αₙ are standard, then α₁+…+αₙ is standard.
2. If α is standard and ω^α > α, then ω^α is standard.
3. If α < Ω and α, β are standard, then Ω^β·α is standard.
4. **If α ∈ C(α), then ψ(α) is standard.**

**Def 5.6 (FS, MOCF with Ω):** Ω's FS is indexed by η < Ω: α = sup_{η<Ω} α[η].
1. If α₁ ≥ … ≥ αₙ: cf(α₁+…+αₙ) = cf(αₙ) and (α₁+…+αₙ)[η] = α₁+…+αₙ[η].
2. cf(ψ(0)) = ω; ψ(0)[0] = ω, ψ(0)[n+1] = ω^{ψ(0)[n]}.
3. cf(ψ(α+1)) = ω; ψ(α+1)[0] = ψ(α), ψ(α+1)[n+1] = ψ(α)^{ψ(α+1)[n]}.
4. cf(Ω) = Ω and **Ω[η] = η**.
5. If cf(α) ≥ ω: cf(Ω^α) = cf(α) and (Ω^α)[η] = Ω^{α[η]}.
6. If cf(β) ≥ ω: cf(Ω^α·β) = cf(β) and (Ω^α·β)[η] = Ω^α·β[η].
7. cf(Ω^α·(β+1)) = cf(Ω^α), and Ω^α·(β+1)[η] = Ω^α·β + Ω^{α}[η] (the book prints Ω^{α[η]}).
8. cf(ε_{Ω+1}) = ω; ε_{Ω+1}[0] = 1, ε_{Ω+1}[n+1] = Ω^{ε_{Ω+1}[n]}.
9. If cf(α) = ω: cf(ψ(α)) = ω and ψ(α)[n] = ψ(α[n]).
10. If cf(α) = Ω: cf(ψ(α)) = ω; **ψ(α)[0] = ψ(α[0]), ψ(α)[n+1] = ψ(α[ψ(α)[n]])**.

Gap (my observation): there is no explicit rule for Ω^{α+1}[η]. The natural one is Ω^{α+1}[η] = Ω^α·η with cofinality Ω, as used implicitly in the examples.

**Def 5.7/5.8 (set-theoretic, with Ω):**
1. C⁰(α) = {0, 1, ω, Ω}.
2. Cⁿ⁺¹(α) = { γ+δ, γ·δ, γ^δ, ψ(η) | γ, δ, η ∈ Cⁿ(α); η < α }.
3. C(α) = ⋃_{n<ω} Cⁿ(α).
4. ψ(α) = min{β | β ∉ C(α)}.

Check: ψ(0) = ε₀ (Cⁿ(0) has max recursive element ω↑↑(n+1)); ψ(Ω) = ζ₀; ψ(Ω+1) = ε_{ζ₀+1}.

**Def 5.9 (standard form with Ωₙ; Ω₀ = ω):**
1. Sums as before.
2. If α < Ωₙ, Ωₙ^β > β, and α, β are standard, then Ωₙ^β·α is standard.
3. If α ∈ Cₙ(α), then ψₙ(α) is standard.

**Def 5.11 (FS with natural-number subscripts; Ω₀ = ω):**
1. Sums: cf = cf(last term), FS goes into the last term.
2. cf(ψₙ(0)) = ω; ψₙ(0)[0] = Ωₙ, ψₙ(0)[n′+1] = Ωₙ^{ψₙ(0)[n′]}.
3. cf(ψₙ(α+1)) = ω; ψₙ(α+1)[0] = ψₙ(α) (book prints ψ(α)), ψₙ(α+1)[n′+1] = ψₙ(α)^{ψₙ(α+1)[n′]}.
4. cf(Ωₙ) = Ωₙ and Ωₙ[η] = η.
5. If cf(α) ≥ ω: cf(Ωₙ^α) = cf(α) and (Ωₙ^α)[η] = Ωₙ^{α[η]}.
6. If cf(β) ≥ ω: cf(Ωₙ^α·β) = cf(β) and (Ωₙ^α·β)[η] = Ωₙ^α·β[η].
7. cf(Ωₙ^α·(β+1)) = cf(Ωₙ^α), and Ωₙ^α·(β+1)[η] = Ωₙ^α·β + (Ωₙ^α)[η].
8. If cf(α) = Ωₙ and m ≤ n: cf(ψ_m(α)) = cf(α) and ψ_m(α)[η] = ψ_m(α[η]) (ψ_m may use α freely).
9. **If cf(α) = Ω_{n+1} and m ≤ n: cf(ψ_m(α)) = ω and ψ_m(α)[n′] = ψ_m(α[γ[n′]]), where γ[0] = 0 and γ[n′+1] = ψₙ(α[γ[n′]]).**

Rule 9 implements find-layer plus renaming: the copied layer is ψₙ = ψ_{(n+1)−1}. The book also says (5.612–5.613) that if cf(α) = ω then the outer layers have cf ω and the [n] passes inward unchanged. (5.613 prints ψ_m(α)[n] = ψ_m(α[0]), which is a typo for α[n].)

Equivalent explicit forms (5.628–5.637), with the innermost Ωₙ replaced by 0 at step [0]:
- \+ : #₀(ψ_m(#+Ωₙ))[0] = #₀(ψ_m(#)); [n′] = n′ nested copies #₀(ψ_m(#+ψ_{n−1}(#+…ψ_{n−1}(#)…))).
- · : [0] = #₀(ψ_m(0)) (note: 0, not #); [n′] = #₀(ψ_m(#·ψ_{n−1}(#·…ψ_{n−1}(0)…))).
- ^ : [0] = #₀(ψ_m(1)); [n′] = #₀(ψ_m(#^{ψ_{n−1}(#^{…ψ_{n−1}(1)})})).
- bare : [0] = #₀(ψ_m(0)); [n′] = #₀(ψ_m(ψ_{n−1}(ψ_{n−1}(…ψ_{n−1}(0)…)))).

The book notes these initial terms differ slightly from the earlier "ψ(#)" convention, but the limits are the same.

### 2.4 Buchholz's OCF (BOCF)
- **Behaviour (5.812–5.815):** ψ(0) = 1; ψ(α+1) = ψ(α)·ω; ψ(α)[n] = ψ(α[n]); and the folding rule ψ(#∗Ω) is the same as in MOCF.
- **Def 5.19 (standard form):**
  1. Non-increasing sums.
  2. If μ is standard, Ω_μ is standard.
  3. If α ∈ C_μ(α), then ψ_μ(α) is standard.

  Only addition is allowed.
- **Def 5.20 (FS, Maksudov's scheme):**
  1. Sums as before.
  2. ψ(0) = 1.
  3. cf(ψ_{ν+1}(0)) = cf(Ω_{ν+1}) and ψ_{ν+1}(0)[η] = η.
  4. If cf(ν) ≥ ω: cf(ψ_ν(0)) = cf(ν) and ψ_ν(0)[η] = ψ_{ν[η]}(0).
  5. cf(ψ_ν(β+1)) = ω; ψ_ν(β+1)[0] = 0, ψ_ν(β+1)[n+1] = ψ_ν(β+1)[n] + ψ_ν(β).
  6. If cf(β) ∈ {ω} ∪ {Ω_{μ+1} | μ < ν} (the book prints ∩, a typo): cf(ψ_ν(β)) = cf(β) and ψ_ν(β)[η] = ψ_ν(β[η]).
  7. If cf(β) = Ω_{μ+1} with μ ≥ ν: cf(ψ_ν(β)) = ω and ψ_ν(β)[n] = ψ_ν(β[γ[n]]), where **γ[0] = Ω_μ** and γ[n+1] = ψ_μ(β[γ[n]]).
- **Def 5.21 (set-theoretic, Maksudov):**
  1. C⁰_ν(α) = {ξ | ξ < Ω_ν}.
  2. C^{m+1}_ν(α) = {γ+δ, ψ_μ(η) | γ, δ, η ∈ C^m_ν(α), μ ∈ Ord, η < α}.
  3. C_ν(α) = ⋃ C^m_ν(α).
  4. ψ_ν(α) = min{β ∉ C_ν(α)}.

  Here Ω₀ = 1, Ω_{ν+1} is the next non-recursive ordinal after Ω_ν, and Ω_ν for limit ν is the sup. Consequences: ψ_ν(0) = Ω_ν and ψ_ν(α+1) = ψ_ν(α)·ω.
- **Buchholz's original (5.901–5.908):**
  - C⁰_ν(α) = Ω_ν.
  - Cⁿ⁺¹_ν(α) = Cⁿ_ν(α) ∪ {γ | P(γ) ⊆ Cⁿ_ν(α)} ∪ {ψ_μ(ξ) | ξ ∈ α ∩ Cⁿ_ν(α) ∧ ξ ∈ C_μ(ξ) ∧ μ ≤ ω}.
  - C_ν(α) = ⋃ₙ Cⁿ_ν(α), and ψ_ν(α) = min{γ | γ ∉ C_ν(α)}.
  - Ω_ν = 1 if ν = 0, and ℵ_ν if ν > 0.
  - P(γ) is the set of additive principal summands {γ₁, …, γ_k} of γ, where P = {α ∈ Ord : 0 < α ∧ ∀ξ,η<α (ξ+η<α)} = {ω^ξ}, and γ = α₁+…+α_k with α₁ ≥ … ≥ α_k ∈ P(γ).

- **SGH:** g₀(n) = 0, g_{α+1}(n) = g_α(n)+1, g_α(n) = g_{α[n]}(n).

### 2.6 PrSS, PSS Hydra, 2-row BMS
**Def 6.1 (PrSS, ordinal version):**
1. () = 0.
2. (#, 0) = (#) + 1.
3. (#₁, aᵢ, #₂, a_k) = (#₁, aᵢ, #₂, aᵢ, #₂, …), where a_k > 0 and **aᵢ = a_k − 1 is the first entry before a_k that is smaller than a_k**. The "…" is the limit of finitely many repetitions.

Terms: aᵢ is the bad root (坏根), "aᵢ, #₂" is the bad part (坏部), #₁ is the good part (好部).

**Def 6.2 (PSS Hydra):**
1. ψ^H₁(0) = 1.
2. #₀(# + ψ^H₁(0)) = #₀(#) + #₀(#) + ⋯, where #₀ and # are any valid expressions. (A trailing p1 inside some function multiplies that function's value by ω. At top level p1 is just +1, e.g. p1+p1 = 2.)
3. **#₀(ψ^H_k(# ψ^H_{k+1}(0))) = #₀(ψ^H_k(# ψ^H_k(# ψ^H_k(# …))))**, where #₀ and # are valid expressions and **# contains no ψ^H_k**. So ψ^H_k is the *first* ψ^H_k found going outward from the innermost ψ^H_{k+1}(0).

All "…" mean the sup of finitely many iterations:
- #₀(#)+#₀(#)+⋯ = sup{#₀(#), #₀(#)+#₀(#), …};
- #₀(ψ^H_k(#ψ^H_k(#…))) = sup{#₀(ψ^H_k(#)), #₀(ψ^H_k(#ψ^H_k(#))), …}.

**Def 6.3 (big-number PSS Hydra):**
1. ψ^H₁(0)[n] = n+1.
2. #₀(#+ψ^H₁(0))[n] = (#₀(#)+#₀(#)+⋯)[n+1].
3. #₀(ψ^H_k(#ψ^H_{k+1}(0)))[n] = #₀(ψ^H_k(#ψ^H_k(#…)))[n+1].

The expansions are taken to finitely many terms (the (n+1)-th term).

**Defs 6.4–6.12 (2-row BMS)**, for the matrix (a₁₁,a₂₁)(a₁₂,a₂₂)…(a₁ₙ,a₂ₙ), where a_ij is row i, column j (1-indexed):
1. **Row-1 parent:** the first entry to the left that is smaller.
2. **Ancestors:** parent, parent's parent, ….
3. **Row-2 parent:** the first entry to the left that is smaller **and whose upper entry is an ancestor of this entry's upper entry**.
4. **Bad root:** the column of the parent of the *lowest non-zero entry of the last column* (scanning bottom-up).
5. **Δ (阶差向量):** last column − bad root, but **its 2nd component is always 0**. If the last column's row-2 entry is 0, Δ's row-1 entry is also 0, so Δ = 0 and the row-2 = 0 case expands exactly like PrSS.
6. **Bad part:** from the bad root (inclusive) to the last column (exclusive).
7. **Good part:** everything before the bad root.

Expansion:
1. (∅) = 0.
2. No bad root (for standard expressions, only when the last column is all 0): value = (matrix minus last column) + 1.
3. Otherwise delete the last column, keep G, and append B, B₁, B₂, … with Bₙ = B + n·Δ, i.e. **G B L ⇒ G B B₁ B₂ …**.

Limit: (0,0)(1,1)(2,2)… = ψ(Ω_ω) = BO.

**Theorem 6.2 (necessary conditions for a standard 2-row BMS):**
1. The first column is all 0.
2. Row 2 ≤ row 1 in each column.
3. Every non-zero entry is ≤ its parent + 1.

**Big-number BMS:** f(n) = S[n]. Expand only to the (n+1)-th term and change [n] to [n+1]; at a successor, drop the last column and change [n] to [n+1]. The value is the bracket number once S is empty. FGH limit ψ(Ω_ω).

### 3.5 PSS Hydra step-by-step (p_k = ψ^H_k, inner (0) omitted; checked against pp.219–221)
- **Expansion examples (6.14–6.17):**
  - p1(p2) → p1(p1(p1(…))). The innermost p2 finds p1, so p1 is copied: terms 1, ω, ω^ω, …, giving ε₀.
  - p1(p2+p2) → p1(p2+p1(p2+p1(p2+…))) = ε₁.
  - p1(p2(p3)+p2) → p1(p2(p3)+p1(p2(p3)+…)).
  - p1(p2(p3+p3)) → p1(p2(p3+p2(p3+p2(…)))). The innermost p3 finds p2 (not p1), so p2(p3+•) is copied.
- **Worked expansion with siblings, (6.270):** p1(p2(p1+p2(p2))) = p1(p2(p1+p2(p1(p2(p1+p2(…)))))). From the innermost p2, go outward: p2 (same subscript, skip), then p2 again (skip), then **p1** (found). The sibling p1 inside is *not* an ancestor, so it is ignored. This corresponds to BMS (0,0)(1,1)(2,0)(2,1)(3,1) → (0,0)(1,1)(2,0)(2,1)(3,0)(4,1)(5,0)(5,1)….

### 3.6 2-row BMS expansions
1. **(0,0)(1,1)(2,2)(3,2)(2,2):**
   - Row-1 ancestors of the last 2 are 1 (col 2) and 0 (col 1).
   - The row-2 parent of 2 is col 2 (a₂₂ = 1 < 2, and a₁₂ is an ancestor), so the bad root is (1,1).
   - G = (0,0), B = (1,1)(2,2)(3,2), Δ = (2,2)−(1,1) = (1,1) → **(1,0)**.
   - ⇒ (0,0)(1,1)(2,2)(3,2) | (2,1)(3,2)(4,2) | (3,1)(4,2)(5,2) | …
   - Its value is ψ(Ω₂+ψ₁(Ω₂+1)) (6.250). The FS terms are:
     - [0] = (0,0)(1,1)(2,2)(3,2) = ψ(Ω₂);
     - [1] = …(2,1)(3,2)(4,2) = ψ(Ω₂+ψ₁(Ω₂)²) (6.246);
     - [2] = …(3,1)(4,2)(5,2) = ψ(Ω₂+ψ₁(Ω₂)^{ψ₁(Ω₂)}) (6.249).

     This is a nice consistency check: ψ₁(Ω₂+1) is a tower of ψ₁(Ω₂)'s.
2. **(0,0)(1,1)(2,0)(2,1)(3,1)** (the book says this is NOT standard and only illustrates the rules):
   - The ancestors of a₁₅ = 3 are a₁₄ = 2, a₁₂ = 1, a₁₁ = 0.
   - For a₂₅ = 1, the candidate a₂₃ = 0 is rejected (a₁₃ = 2 is not an ancestor) and a₂₁ = 0 is accepted. So the bad root is column 1, G = ∅, B = the first 4 columns, Δ = (3,0).
   - ⇒ (0,0)(1,1)(2,0)(2,1)(3,0)(4,1)(5,0)(5,1)(6,0)(7,1)(8,0)(8,1)…
   - Book slip: it says "Δ = 坏部与好部的差值"; it is last column − bad root.
3. **(0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)(2,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1):** the bad root is column 1, B = all columns except the last, Δ = (3,0). ⇒ …(3,0)(4,1)(5,1)(6,1)(5,0)(4,1)(5,1)…
4. **(0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)(2,0):** row 2 of the last column is 0, so only row 1 matters (PrSS-like). The parent of 2 is 1 (col 2), so the bad root is (1,1), B = (1,1)(2,1)(3,1), Δ = 0. ⇒ (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)…
5. **Successor:** (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(0,0) = (0,0)(1,1)(2,1) + 1.
6. **(0,0)(1,1) = (0,0)(1,0)(2,0)(3,0)… = (0)(1)(2)(3)… = ε₀.** This is the 1-row limit.
7. **Standardness derivation (6.144):** limit → (0,0)(1,1)(2,2) → (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)(4,1) → … → (0,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1)(2,0)(1,1)(2,1)(3,1).

## 5. Rigour issues and pitfalls (as pointed out by the book, plus typos I found)

### Conceptual (the book's own warnings)
1. **Counting from 1.** α_β is the "(1+β)-th" fixed point. Zero-based counting would be cleaner, but convention is kept. Students often get ϕ(1,β) = ε_β off by one.
2. **A limit point is not always a fixed point.** You need continuity, f(α₀)[n] = f(α₀[n]). Theorem 4.1 gives the conditions (strictly increasing + continuous).
3. **The choice of FS matters for FGH and SGH, not for the ordinal's value.** There are two valid FSs of ε_{α+1} that interleave, and the book proves both have the same sup. SGH is especially FS-sensitive.
4. **ϕ(ω, ϕ(ω,0)+1) ≠ sup ϕ(n, ϕ(ω,0)+1).** Don't confuse "next common fixed point" with plugging into the second argument.
5. **Overestimation risk** around ϕ(1,0,0) → ϕ(1,0,1) (footnote 5).
6. **η₀ name clash:** proof theory sometimes uses η₀ = BHO, while googology uses η₀ = ϕ(3,0).
7. **Ordinal "positions" in ϕ(α@β) are not real positions.** Only finitely many non-zero terms can be written.
8. **Extended Veblen rules are not standardised.** The book uses 梅天狸's version.
9. **Transfinite extensions are weak.** SVO→LVO and BO→EBO look large but are small gains. Real progress needs a new recursive core.
10. **What Ω is:**
    - The book's Ω is the first *non-recursive* ordinal (ω₁^CK), not ω₁. Older definitions use ω₁, which is "too powerful".
    - Why it's fine: only the fact that Ω is unreachable from below is used. For Ω-only MOCF even Ω = BHO would work. The non-recursive choice future-proofs extensions.
    - The chapter blurs "recursive ordinal" with "recursive ordinal < Ω".
    - Ω_ω and Ω_α for limit α are actually *not* non-recursive (they have recursive FSs Ω_α[n]).
11. **Recursive cofinality vs true cofinality:** Ω has recursive cofinality Ω but true cofinality ω, because the ω-sequence isn't recursive. MOCF FS definitions use cf in the "recursive" sense, indexing Ω's FS by η < Ω.
12. **An OCF is not really a recursive notation.** Googology "OCFs" are recursive notations that mimic collapsing behaviour. The real definition is set-theoretic, and the book gives both and argues they agree.
13. **Above ψ(Ω_{Ω_{…}}), OCF usage is chaotic** and there is no unified version. Proof-theoretic OCFs are rigorous but rarely understood by googologists.
14. **Standard forms matter:**
    - ψ(α) is standard only if α ∈ C(α).
    - BOCF standard form uses only +.
    - BMS / PSS Hydra "standard" means reachable from the limit by taking FSs.
    - The necessary conditions (Thm 6.1, 6.2) are not sufficient. "Valid" (合法) is a strictly wider class.
    - Some example expressions in the book are explicitly nonstandard, e.g. (0,0)(1,1)(2,0)(2,1)(3,1).
15. **Well-ordering / the plateau:** ψ is *not* injective or strictly increasing on all arguments. ψ(α) = ζ₀ for every recursive α > ζ₀, and in general ψ(α) is constant on (β, next Ω-term]. ψ(Ω) = ψ(ζ₀) shows the first collapse value equals the plateau.
16. **MOCF initial-term conventions differ between sections:**
    - Def 5.1: ψ(α+1)[0] = 0. Def 5.6: ψ(α+1)[0] = ψ(α) and ψ(0)[0] = ω.
    - Explicit rules 5.628–5.637: [0] replaces Ωₙ with 0 (or 1 for ^).
    - Eq 5.213 vs 5.341: ψ(Ω^Ω)[0] = ψ(0) vs ψ(Ω).
    - The limits are the same, but **pick one convention for a course and say so.**
17. **BOCF vs MOCF conventions:** the same symbol ψ(Ω) is ζ₀ in MOCF and ε₀ in BOCF. Always label which OCF is meant. The book defaults to MOCF.
18. **Buchholz's original BOCF uses ℵ_ν (uncountable cardinals) and μ ≤ ω.** The book's recursive and set-theoretic versions follow Maksudov.

## 6. Open problems / research directions mentioned or implied

The book doesn't list formal open problems in this part. It flags these unsettled areas and forward pointers:
1. **No unified OCF beyond ψ(Ω_{Ω_{…}})/EBO.** "Everyone uses different collapsing rules." Making googology-style OCFs rigorous, and connecting them to proof-theoretic OCFs, is open work.
2. **Recursive OCF notations vs genuine set-theoretic OCFs.** Recursive OCF notations only "look like" OCFs. Proving that a recursive rule system matches its set-theoretic definition is done informally here, through enumeration.
3. **Reflection ordinals** (反射序数): the recursively inaccessible I and stronger ones, used in OCFs. Deferred to a later chapter. ψ^B(I) = EBO, and I behaves like a successor-subscripted Ω.
4. **The FGH/SGH catching function C(α).** Values are known for small α, e.g. C(ω) = ψ(ψ_I(0)). It is only meaningful for recursive ordinals, and its general behaviour is a research topic.
5. **The FGH–OCF relation in category theory** (ref. [922]).
6. **Multi-row BMS:** with the right rules it becomes an "extremely strong" notation (later chapters). Its well-foundedness and termination are famous issues in the community (not stated in this part).
7. **Fine FGH analysis depends on the chosen fundamental sequences.** Comparing notations precisely (e.g. ε_{α+1} with two FSs, or SGH values) is delicate.
8. **"Beyond the ellipsis":** recursive ordinals beyond BHO have structure "far beyond naive imagination". The book's agenda is to find stronger *recursive cores* (Hydra / Worm structures) rather than transfinite generalisations.

## 7. Recommended minimal path for a high-school newcomer: ε₀ → Γ₀ → SVO/LVO → BHO → BO

### Prerequisite chain (from Part I)
1. Ordinals as "positions after counting forever": successor vs limit, ω, ordinal +, ·, ^ (non-commutativity, 1+ω = ω).
2. Fundamental sequences α[n] and the idea that limits = sup of FS.
3. Cantor-normal-form style standard forms below ε₀, and ε₀ = ω^ω^ω^….
4. FGH f_α(n), for motivation: "bigger ordinal → faster function". PrSS (初等序列) as a sequence game with limit ε₀ (helps hugely in Ch.6).

### Skip or defer for a newcomer
- **Second ε definition and its equivalence proof (4.2):** optional enrichment. Mention only that FS choice affects FGH.
- **Full Def 4.17 and Def 4.19 rule lists:** show the unified fixed-point sentences instead. Skip 4.6 (extended position) and 4.7 (weak Veblen) apart from the remark "weak Veblen catches Veblen at BHO".
- **Recursive cofinality machinery (Def 5.4–5.6, 5.10–5.11, 5.15–5.16):** keep it for a "for the rigorous reader" appendix. The informal folding rule is enough to compute.
- **5.5 ordinal subscripts:** just name TFBO, BIO and EBO, plus the lesson "transfinite generalisation ≈ small gain".
- **5.6 BOCF:** only show that ψ^B(Ω) = ε₀ and ψ^B(Ω₂) = BHO. That is enough to read other sources such as Wikipedia, which mostly use Buchholz/BOCF conventions.
- **5.7 (BOCF/MOCF catching) and 5.8 (FGH/SGH catching):** optional "wow" moments. The runner analogy (2·ω = ω) and the headline f_BO ≈ g_BO are accessible.

### Suggested pedagogical order of milestones
ε₀ (towers) → ε_α (jump out with +1) → ζ₀ (fixed points of ε) → ϕ(α,β) and Γ₀ (fixed points of the first argument) → odometer to SVO, then LVO (variables at ordinal positions) → "we're stuck, so invent Ω" → ψ plateau at ζ₀ → Ω-folding: re-derive ζ₀, η₀, Γ₀, SVO, LVO in one uniform language → BHO = ψ(ε_{Ω+1}) → Ω₂, ψ₁ and renaming → BO = ψ(Ω_ω) → the same thing as a sequence game: PSS Hydra / (0,0)(1,1)(2,2)….

**Recurring motif to emphasise:** whenever a process gets stuck at a limit, *name the limit and promote it* (ω, ε, ϕ-levels, Ω, Ω₂, …). Then the find-root-and-copy pattern (PrSS → PSS → BMS) is the clean recursive core of that idea.
